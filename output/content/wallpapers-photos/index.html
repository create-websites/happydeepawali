Why the timing matters

Worship with faith, preparation and presence

Many households look for the overlap of Pradosh Kaal, Amavasya Tithi and Vrishabha Lagna for the principal Lakshmi Puja. Regional practices can use different windows and sequences.

Simple approach: prepare the thali and altar in advance, begin at your chosen local muhurat, and follow the family or temple Puja Vidhi with care.

For exact sunrise, tithi boundaries and festival observance outside India, consult a regional Panchang.

Complete home worship guide

Lakshmi–Ganesha Puja Vidhi for Diwali 2026

A respectful, family-friendly sequence to help you prepare, understand the meaning of each offering, and perform Puja with attention. Customs and mantras vary by sampradaya, region and household.

Begin with a clean space and a peaceful mind

Many families worship Shri Ganesha and Maa Lakshmi together on Diwali, asking for wisdom, well-being and prosperity used with care. Keep the ritual simple if you are new to Puja; sincerity matters more than an elaborate arrangement.

When to begin: use the city-specific Muhurat above. Prepare the altar, thali and offerings before the evening window so you are not rushed.

The worship sequence

12 steps for a meaningful Lakshmi Puja

Tap a step to read the instructions
01Cleanse & prepare the altar⌄

Clean the worship area, lay a fresh cloth over a stable chowki, and arrange the murti or image of Maa Lakshmi and Shri Ganesha. Keep a safely placed diya and offerings ready.

02Invoke peace and make Sankalpa⌄

Wash your hands, sit comfortably, take a quiet moment and state your intention: gratitude, wisdom, health and the welfare of all. A short prayer in your own language is appropriate.

03Begin with Shri Ganesha⌄

Offer a flower and pray to Lord Ganesha for clarity and the removal of obstacles. The simple mantra “ॐ गं गणपतये नमः” may be recited.

04Place the Kalash, if customary⌄

Some families set a water-filled kalash with leaves and a coconut as a symbol of auspiciousness and abundance. Follow your household custom for its arrangement.

05Welcome Maa Lakshmi⌄

Invoke Maa Lakshmi respectfully with folded hands, flowers and a gentle prayer. If using delicate or painted idols, avoid pouring liquids directly onto them.

06Offer fragrance, color and flowers⌄

Present sandal paste or kumkum, akshata and fresh blossoms as appropriate for the deity and tradition. Each offering expresses care and reverence.

07Offer Dhupa and Deepa⌄

Light incense or dhoop only where safe and ventilated, then offer a diya. Let its light symbolize the removal of ignorance, fear and harshness.

08Present Naivedya and water⌄

Offer fruit, mithai, homemade food or other satvik offerings suitable to your tradition, with a small cup of drinking water. Keep the offerings clean and covered.

09Recite mantra or stotra⌄

Chant “ॐ श्रीं महालक्ष्म्यै नमः”, read a family prayer, or recite Shri Sukta where customary. If Sanskrit is unfamiliar, a sincere prayer in your mother tongue is welcome.

10Perform Ganesha and Lakshmi Aarti⌄

Offer the aarti light respectfully, sing the traditional aarti or a familiar devotional verse, and pray for insight, compassion and prosperity.

11Share Prasad and blessings⌄

Bow in gratitude, offer namaskar to elders, share prasad with everyone present and include neighbors or those celebrating alone when possible.

12Close with gratitude and care⌄

Safely extinguish or monitor diyas, keep the puja space tidy and carry forward the spirit of generosity. A simple donation or act of service makes the celebration meaningful.

Do I need a full Shodashopachara (16-step) Puja?

Some traditions use sixteen formal forms of hospitality to the deity, including avahana, asana, achamana, snana, vastra, gandha, pushpa, dhoop, deep and naivedya. For full Vedic or temple-style observances, use a trusted family Puja book or seek guidance from a qualified priest. The 12-step overview above is a beginner-friendly home guide, not a substitute for lineage-specific directions.

Can I perform Diwali Puja without a priest?

Many families perform a simple home Puja themselves. Use familiar prayers, make offerings respectfully and be mindful of local Muhurat. A priest can help when a specialized rite, business account book ceremony or formal havan is planned.

What if I am celebrating away from India?

Use the local Panchang where the Puja is happening rather than simply converting an Indian clock time. Keep the observance practical for your space, family and fire-safety rules. Traditions may vary from city to city.

Questions people ask

Diwali 2026 frequently asked questions

Dates, customs and muhurats can change with location. Here are the essentials for planning with clarity.

When is Diwali 2026?

In New Delhi, Diwali and Lakshmi Puja fall on Sunday, November 8, 2026. The precise Puja Muhurat varies by location and local Panchang.

What are the main Diwali festival dates in 2026?

Dhanteras is November 6, Kali Chaudas November 7, Diwali November 8, Govardhan Puja November 10, and Bhai Dooj November 11 in the New Delhi reference calendar. Some regional observances differ.

Is Govardhan Puja on November 9 or November 10 in 2026?

For New Delhi in 2026, Govardhan Puja is observed on Tuesday, November 10. The Pratipada Tithi begins on November 9; a tithi start date is not automatically the festival observance date.

Can I use the New Delhi Lakshmi Puja time abroad?

No. The evening muhurat depends on local sunset, Pradosh Kaal, Amavasya and often Vrishabha Lagna. Check the location-specific time for the place of worship.

How do I perform a simple Lakshmi Puja at home?

Prepare a clean puja space, gather offerings, make a sankalpa, offer Ganesha prayers, honor Maa Lakshmi with flowers, diya, naivedya and mantra or aarti, then share prasad. Follow your family or temple tradition for detailed rites.

How is Dev Deepawali different from Diwali?

Diwali is celebrated around Amavasya. Dev Deepawali takes place on Kartik Purnima, and the Varanasi celebration is especially associated with deep daan along the River Ganga.
