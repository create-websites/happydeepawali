Dussehra Worksheet for Kids: Rama, Ravana, Durga and Traditions

Discover the Dussehra Worksheet for Kids: Learn about Rama, Ravana, Durga, and Traditions, including cultural background, regional variations, practical tips, FAQs, and more.

Printable-style worksheet prompts

  1. Write one sentence explaining Dussehra.
  2. Assign Rama, Sita, Hanuman, and Ravana to their respective roles in the Ramayana.
  3. Name one Shakti tradition connected with Vijayadashami.
  4. Identify the traditions that are specific to certain regions instead of being practiced universally: Ravana Dahan, Ayudha Puja, Vidyarambham, Bijoya Dashami.
  5. Compose three polite questions you would pose to someone regarding their family’s festival customs.
  6. Create a secure festival poster featuring a message centered on bravery or new beginnings.

Cultural context and respectful participation

Dussehra is better understood as a collection of interconnected Vijayadashami customs rather than a uniform practice observed by all Hindu families. A visitor might experience a Ramayana-based Ramlila performance in one city, a Durga Puja send-off in another, Ayudha Puja or Saraswati rituals in the South, Maharashtrian Shami and Apta ceremonies in the West, or unique deity-focused celebrations in regions like Kullu and Bastar. These variations contribute to the festival’s cultural diversity, rather than being anomalies that must be unified under a single interpretation.

When attending a celebration outside your own tradition, watch carefully and seek permission before taking part in any ritual. Respect temple and venue guidelines, refrain from disrupting prayers or processions for photos, and refer to local customs by their proper names. In schools, workplaces, or public materials, do not portray Ravana Dahan, Sindoor Khela, Vidyarambham, or other regional practices as activities common to all Dussehra celebrations.

Practical planning notes

  • Refer to a location-specific panchang when the precise Vijayadashami tithi or muhurat is important.
  • For public events, confirm the current organizer, location, entry requirements, and schedule rather than depending on outdated information.
  • Arrange transportation and accessibility in advance for major fairs, processions, and Ramlila venues.
  • Ensure children are kept away from fire, fireworks, crowded edges, and temporary electrical setups.
  • Choose reusable decorations and lower-waste serving options where practical.
  • For family gatherings, tailor the day to match your household’s regional customs and the degree of observance you genuinely adhere to.

Frequently asked questions

What is Dussehra?

Dussehra is an important Hindu festival linked to Vijayadashami. Various regions highlight Rama’s triumph over Ravana, Durga’s defeat of Mahishasura, royal Dasara ceremonies, educational rituals, or other regional practices.

Is Dussehra the same as Vijayadashami?

The terms are frequently applied to the same festival day, though regional preferences and ritual focus vary.

When is Dussehra celebrated?

It is celebrated on Dashami following Navratri based on the Hindu lunar calendar. The corresponding Gregorian date varies annually.

Why is Ravana burned?

In numerous North Indian customs, Ravana Dahan enacts Rama’s conquest of Ravana and symbolizes the victory of dharma over evil.
