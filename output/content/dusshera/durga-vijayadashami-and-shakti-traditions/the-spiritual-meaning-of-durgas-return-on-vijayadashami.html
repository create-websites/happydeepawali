The Spiritual Meaning of Durga’s Return on Vijayadashami

Discover the spiritual significance of Durga’s return on Vijayadashami, including cultural background, regional variations, practical tips, FAQs, and connections to Dussehra and related traditions.

The spiritual significance of Durga’s return on Vijayadashami is most clearly grasped within the broader context of the Dussehra and Vijayadashami season, during which the festival day holds various stories and customs across India and its diaspora. This guide highlights the spiritual meaning of Durga’s return on Vijayadashami, while distinguishing local customs from those more commonly observed.

In numerous North Indian contexts, Dussehra is linked to the Ramayana, Ramlila, and Rama’s triumph over Ravana. Within Shakta traditions, Vijayadashami signifies Durga’s defeat of Mahishasura and the end of Durga Puja. States like Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Himachal Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and others contribute their unique customs.

The Shakti and Durga Puja context

In goddess-focused traditions, Vijayadashami comes after the key days of Durga Puja and signifies the festival’s end. Durga’s defeat of Mahishasura symbolizes the triumph of divine power over a harmful force, while the last day also embodies feelings of farewell and departure.

Bijoya Dashami customs

  • Offering pranam or respectful greetings to elders
  • Exchanging Shubho Bijoya wishes
  • Visiting relatives and friends
  • Sharing sweets or snacks
  • Participating in or observing Durga Visarjan according to local practice

Regional precision matters

Dussehra, Vijayadashami, and Bijoya Dashami coincide on the calendar, yet their cultural significance varies across regions. The Bengali farewell to Durga Puja should not be seen simply as a variant of Ravana Dahan, just as the North Indian Ramlila should not be characterized as a Durga Puja practice.

Cultural context and respectful participation

Dussehra is better understood as a collection of interconnected Vijayadashami customs rather than a uniform ritual practiced by all Hindu families. One might witness a Ramayana-based Ramlila performance in one city, a Durga Puja send-off in another, Ayudha Puja or Saraswati celebrations in the South, Maharashtrian Shami and Apta observances in the West, or unique deity-focused rituals in regions like Kullu and Bastar. These variations contribute to the festival’s cultural diversity and should be seen as integral aspects, not anomalies requiring a single interpretation.

When attending a celebration outside your own tradition, watch carefully and seek permission before taking part in any rituals. Adhere to the rules of temples and venues, refrain from interrupting prayers or processions to take photos, and refer to local customs by their proper names. In schools, workplaces, or public materials, avoid portraying Ravana Dahan, Sindoor Khela, Vidyarambham, or any other regional practices as universally part of every Dussehra celebration.

Practical planning notes

  • For precise determination of Vijayadashami tithi or muhurat, refer to a panchang specific to your location.
  • For public events, confirm the current organizer, location, entry requirements, and schedule rather than depending on outdated information.
  • Arrange transportation and accessibility well in advance for major fairs, processions, and Ramlila venues.
  • Ensure children are kept away from fire, fireworks, crowded edges, and temporary electrical setups.
  • Choose reusable decorations and lower-waste serving options where practical.
  • For family gatherings, tailor the day to the local customs and the degree of observance your household genuinely practices.

Frequently asked questions

What is Dussehra?

Dussehra is an important Hindu festival linked to Vijayadashami. Various regions highlight Rama’s triumph over Ravana, Durga’s defeat of Mahishasura, royal Dasara ceremonies, educational rituals, or other regional traditions.

Is Dussehra the same as Vijayadashami?

The terms are frequently applied to the same festival day, though regional preferences and ritual focus vary.

When is Dussehra celebrated?

It is celebrated on Dashami following Navratri as per the Hindu lunar calendar. The corresponding Gregorian date varies annually.

Why is Ravana burned?

In numerous North Indian customs, Ravana Dahan depicts Rama’s conquest of Ravana and symbolizes the victory of dharma over evil.
