The Dual Mythology of Dussehra: Rama and Durga

Examine the combination of Rama's triumph over Ravana and Durga's conquest of Mahishasura during Vijayadashami, showcasing the strong traditions of righteousness and rejuvenation.

Why this guide matters: Dussehra does not revolve around a single narrative. The variations in how the festival is celebrated across different regions can be attributed to the incorporation of both the Ramayana and Shakta traditions.

Two victory stories, one festival

In north India, Dussehra marks the end of the Ramayana cycle as Lord Rama triumphs over Ravana. Similarly, in eastern and Shakta traditions, Vijayadashami concludes Durga Puja by honoring Goddess Durga's victory over Mahishasura. Both stories signify the festival's theme of restoring order and balance.

The differing narratives make each story unique rather than interchangeable, serving as a reminder that Indian festivals often blend various regional traditions on one sacred day.

Rama, Ravana and the public drama of Dussehra

Ramlila shows recount tales from the Ramayana before Dussehra, culminating in the burning of Ravana effigies as a powerful reminder: unchecked power leads to self-destruction.

For families, the focus is often on reflecting on courage, loyalty, duty, and the repercussions of pride rather than celebrating the defeat of an enemy.

Durga, Mahishasura and Vijayadashami

In Eastern regions like Bengal, the emotional journey during Durga Puja varies. The festival honors the Goddess as a nurturing and protective figure, with Vijayadashami symbolizing both triumph and bidding farewell as her image is prepared for immersion.

The defeat of Mahishasura is not only a triumph of strength, but also a celebration of community, homecoming, farewell, and the promise of reunion.

What unites the traditions

The common theme is the reestablishment of dharma. Evil is not depicted solely as an external entity; the narratives prompt contemplation on ego, anger, injustice, fear, and abuse of authority.

This is why Vijayadashami continues to be relevant in the modern world, as it can be both religious, dramatic, communal, and intimate all at once.

Common questions

Are Dussehra and Vijayadashami the same festival?

They typically mention the same tenth-day peak, despite variations in regional traditions and titles.

Why are both Rama and Durga associated with the day?

Various regional customs associate Vijayadashami with diverse sacred stories of triumph and rejuvenation.

Is Ravana Dahan practiced everywhere?

No, it plays a significant role in various north Indian festivities, with different regions highlighting Durga immersion, processions, Shastra Puja, or worship of local deities.
