Conquering the Ravana Within: A Dussehra Self-Reflection Guide

Leverage Dussehra to assess and improve anger, procrastination, ego, distraction, and other habits that subtly influence your daily routine.

Why this guide matters: Ravana Dahan is more than just labeling others as evil; it serves as a reminder to recognize harmful patterns within ourselves.

Move the metaphor inward

Public effigy burning is attention-grabbing, but personal growth happens in silence. Look for tangible behaviors like yelling when under pressure, procrastinating on challenging tasks, constant self-comparison, excessive spending for validation, or a reluctance to admit mistakes.

It is easier to modify specific behaviors than vague goals like "improve."

Name one trigger

Identify the triggering situation for each habit. Feeling ignored can lead to anger; ambiguity in tasks can lead to procrastination; social-media use can intensify feelings of comparison.

The trigger is not an excuse. It is useful data.

Design a counter-practice

Pair every design with a compact option. Take ten deep breaths before responding in anger. Begin a ten-minute segment on a postponed project. Stop following sources of comparison. Implement a cooling-off period before significant buys.

A festival resolution is truly achieved only if it results in a lasting change in behavior post-festival.

Create a ten-day follow-through

Jot down one goal on a note card and commit to a concrete action plan for the next ten days. Evaluate successes, failures, and areas for improvement.

The aim is not to achieve perfection. The lasting impact of the Dussehra message comes from striving for self-improvement through continual correction.

Common questions

Do I need to list ten personal vices?

Having one honest habit is more beneficial than having ten abstract labels.

How can families do this together?

Every individual has the option to select a personal habit to work on in private or to share a positive practice they wish to improve.

Is this a religious ritual?

This contemporary reflective activity draws inspiration from the symbolism of Dussehra, without intending to replace traditional worship practices.
