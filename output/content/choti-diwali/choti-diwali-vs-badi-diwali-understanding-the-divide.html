Choti Diwali vs. Badi Diwali: Understanding the Divide

Choti Diwali and Badi Diwali are part of the same festival period, yet the terms do not denote the exact same timing across all communities.

Article 7 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Stories & significance/Article 7

Choti Diwali and Badi Diwali are part of the same festival period, yet the terms do not denote the exact same timing across all communities. In much of North India, Choti Diwali suggests Naraka Chaturdashi and preparation, while Badi Diwali points to the principal evening of lamps and Lakshmi Puja.

Compare the focus

Choti Diwali typically emphasizes an early bath, home preparations, and local customs of protection or remembrance. The primary Lakshmi Puja evening usually focuses on Lakshmi and Ganesha, family prayers, guests, and a more elaborate celebration. These represent general practices, not strict measures of religious accuracy.

Allow for calendar overlap

The two observances do not have to fall on different civil dates. The lunar period controlling a dawn bath and that governing evening worship may happen on the same day. A festival calendar ought to specify each activity and its corresponding rule instead of merely numbering the days.

Recognize southern Deepavali

For numerous Tamil families, the early morning of Deepavali is the central occasion, marked by bathing, wearing fresh clothes, exchanging greetings, and enjoying food. Describing this as simply a prelude to a northern evening celebration would underestimate its significance.

Plan the work sensibly

Take advantage of the quieter time to review supplies and prepare food that keeps well. Set aside moments for relaxation and family talks. A substantial second shopping trip or a second full feast is optional, not a requirement dictated by the terms small and big.
