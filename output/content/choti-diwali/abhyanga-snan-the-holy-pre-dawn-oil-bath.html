Abhyanga Snan: The Holy Pre-Dawn Oil Bath

Article 12 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Bathing & renewal/Article 12

Abhyanga Snan is the traditional oil bath ritual linked to Naraka Chaturdashi in numerous customs. Taking place before dawn, this practice is particularly helpful for preparation: the goal is to begin the day calmly, then don fresh clothes, exchange greetings, and continue with the household ceremonies.

Prepare the evening before

Consult the local calendar for the designated bathing period, and prepare towels, a small quantity of familiar oil, and clean clothes. Ensure the bathroom floor remains unobstructed. Any individual requiring help should have support organized in advance, rather than relying on a hurried or tired family member.

Keep the sequence simple

A household might start with prayer, use a small amount of oil, enjoy a short massage, and then take a bath. Sesame oil is traditionally linked to this practice, though family traditions vary. The oil doesn’t have to be warm, strongly scented, or used in large amounts to be significant.

Make comfort part of the practice

Maintain water at a comfortable warmth and promptly eliminate oily residues from surfaces. Refrain from using unfamiliar mixtures on irritated skin. If someone finds the traditional routine difficult, they can opt for a simpler bath and still participate in the rest of the celebration.

Close with a moment of attention

Put on clean clothes and greet others before returning to preparations. Write down the family's preferred sequence afterwards so the next generation inherits a clear account rather than a list of hurried instructions.
