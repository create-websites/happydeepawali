How to Make the Perfect Traditional Ubtan

Article 13 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Bathing & renewal/Article 13

Traditional ubtan is a freshly prepared paste employed in certain celebratory bathing rituals. Typical home-made versions consist of gram flour combined with a pinch of turmeric and water or another familiar liquid. Recipes differ, and there is no specific mixture required for Choti Diwali.

Mix only what you need

To prepare a simple household mixture, mix one tablespoon of fine gram flour with a small pinch of turmeric and add enough plain water to create a smooth paste. This serves as a cultural recipe example and is not intended for treating any skin condition. Ensure utensils are clean and prepare a fresh small batch each time.

Choose simplicity over novelty

Some family recipes include sandalwood, rose water, and dairy components, but increasing the number of ingredients doesn’t improve the paste. Fragrances, spices, and food items can irritate the skin. If an ingredient has previously triggered a reaction, omit it instead of strictly adhering to the recipe.

Apply without scrubbing

Anyone opting to use ubtan should avoid contact with the eyes and broken skin, refrain from harsh scrubbing, and rinse it off gently. Discontinue use if irritation develops. A gentle cleanser and simple bath are suitable alternatives, particularly for those with sensitive skin.

Preserve the family recipe thoughtfully

Document who shared the recipe, its ingredients, and the significance associated with it. Maintain modesty in cosmetic claims: appreciating a ritual differs from demonstrating that a paste detoxifies, whitens, or medically enhances the skin.
