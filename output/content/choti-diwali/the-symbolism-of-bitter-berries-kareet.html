The Symbolism of Bitter Berries (Kareet)

In certain regions of Maharashtra and Goa, a bitter fruit called kareet or karit locally is featured in Naraka Chaturdashi traditions.

Article 10 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Stories & significance/Article 10

In certain regions of Maharashtra and Goa, a bitter fruit called kareet or karit locally is featured in Naraka Chaturdashi traditions. Crushing it symbolically recalls the defeat of Narakasura. The act belongs to local tradition and should not become a universal instruction for every family.

Explain the symbolic gesture

Bitterness serves as a powerful metaphor for anger, pride, or cruelty. A family might associate the action with letting go of an unhelpful habit. The spiritual meaning holds greater importance than putting on a theatrical display or demanding a precise number of repetitions.

Follow a known local custom

Names, botanical classifications, and the specifics of the gesture differ. Avoid identifying a wild fruit solely by its appearance or using an unfamiliar berry found outside. Consult a knowledgeable local vendor or relative to determine if the custom is observed in your household.

Keep children involved safely

An adult can manage the fruit and narrate the story. Children, on the other hand, can draw Narakasura and write about a kind act they wish to perform. There is no requirement to taste the fruit, touch the seeds, or imitate unfamiliar actions seen in an online video.

Adapt without anxiety

If the fruit is not available, share the story and light a lamp under supervision. Being far from a local market does not hinder a meaningful celebration of bravery, humility, and renewal.
