Why It's Called Naraka Chaturdashi

The term Naraka Chaturdashi links a tale of liberation with the fourteenth day of the lunar cycle. Chaturdashi refers to the fourteenth tithi; Naraka relates to both…

Article 3 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Stories & significance/Article 3

The term Naraka Chaturdashi links a tale of liberation with the fourteenth day of the lunar cycle. Chaturdashi denotes the fourteenth tithi; Naraka relates both to Narakasura and, in ritual terminology, to the domain of suffering. This name encompasses multiple layers of meaning rather than a straightforward translation.

Understand the lunar setting

The observance takes place during the waning fortnight around Diwali. Month names vary between the amanta and purnimanta calendar systems: Ashwin and Kartik may refer to the same seasonal event in different frameworks. A variation in the month name does not necessarily indicate a dispute over the festival.

Separate belief from a prediction

Traditional bathing prayers often include a desire to be released from Naraka. This devotional language focuses on purification and well-being. It should not be interpreted as a warning that failing to bathe will lead to a specific punishment.

Notice why dates can overlap

A tithi starts and ends based on the shifting angular position between the Moon and the Sun. Its limits do not correspond to midnight. As a result, a pre-dawn bath and an evening celebration may be scheduled differently depending on the year or place.

Explain the name at home

Employ a straightforward three-part framework: a lunar day, a celebrated victory, and a desire to leave behind harmful habits. This approach maintains clarity while respecting the uniqueness of regional traditions without reducing them to a single set of rules.
