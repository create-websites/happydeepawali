Kali Chaudas: Tantric Traditions in Gujarat and Rajasthan

Article 21 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Regional traditions/Article 21

Kali Chaudas is a ritual primarily observed in Gujarat and certain areas of western India. The prayers and narratives linked to it often emphasize protection, bravery, and facing fear. It is best understood with regional specificity rather than as a uniform ceremony practiced by all families.

Distinguish the observances

Calendars that separate Kali Chaudas from Naraka Chaturdashi apply a night-based timing rule. The resulting civil date may differ from the pre-dawn bath observance. Bengal's Kali Puja, associated with Amavasya, is also a distinct festival setting.

Understand specialist traditions respectfully

Certain reports address esoteric rituals, local guardian entities, or sites associated with death. These are specific to particular traditions and communities. A general household guide ought to acknowledge their presence without offering guidance on unfamiliar or potentially dangerous practices.

Keep household worship familiar

A family might select a lamp, a devotional reading, or a visit to their regular temple. Inquire at that temple about its current schedule. There is no requirement to go to a remote location at night or to play a video in order to take part meaningfully.

Speak about protection without fear

Introduce current worries about harmful forces within the context of religious beliefs and folklore. Refrain from implying that common misfortunes confirm the existence of black magic. Offering a gentle explanation of bravery and kindness provides children with a more positive approach to the tradition.
