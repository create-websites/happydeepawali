The Astrological Significance of Chaturdashi Tithi

Chaturdashi timing is rooted in the Hindu lunar calendar. A tithi tracks a twelve-degree interval in the Moon's angular separation from the Sun.

Article 9 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Stories & significance/Article 9

Chaturdashi timing is rooted in the Hindu lunar calendar. A tithi tracks a twelve-degree interval in the Moon's angular separation from the Sun. Its length and its placement within a civil date vary, which explains why a single fixed clock time cannot serve every Choti Diwali observance.

Astronomy and interpretation

The tithi is determined by the relationship between the Sun and the Moon. Religious calendars then establish observance guidelines based on dawn, sunset, or other specific times of the day. Wider astrological interpretations are part of belief systems and should be separated from the actual astronomical calculations.

Ask which ritual you are timing

Abhyanga Snan is linked to the time before sunrise. Kali Chaudas follows a distinct nighttime guideline in calendars that sets it apart. Lakshmi Puja has specific evening timings. A time borrowed from one ritual should not be applied to another.

Read a local panchang carefully

Choose the city, year, and time zone, then review both the tithi interval and the specified ritual window. Verify if the shown times go past midnight. Families living overseas should use their local time instead of simply converting an Indian clock time mechanically.

Keep a useful planning note

Include the activity, local date, start and end times, city, and source together. This brief note helps avoid confusion when family members share screenshots without location enabled.
