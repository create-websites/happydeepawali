Deepavali in Tamil Nadu: The Main Event

For numerous Tamil households, Deepavali starts early in the morning with an oil bath, wearing clean or new clothes, exchanging greetings, and enjoying festive food.

Article 25 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Regional traditions/Article 25

For numerous Tamil households, Deepavali starts early in the morning with an oil bath, wearing clean or new clothes, exchanging greetings, and enjoying festive food. This morning is central to the celebration. It should not be described simply as a smaller preliminary version of North Indian Diwali.

Prepare for the early start

Arrange bathing supplies and clothing, and determine which meals can be made ahead of time. After bathing, family members might welcome elders and share blessings. Since the order differs, consult the host family instead of assuming a standard procedure.

Make food part of the welcome

Sweets and savouries make for a plentiful spread, whether homemade, brought by relatives, or store-bought. Begin with smaller portions so guests can sample a variety of treats. Someone arriving later in the morning can still join in without needing to stick to the original timetable.

Explain the story locally

Narakasura's defeat is a familiar theme, but Tamil Deepavali includes its own family memories and devotional expressions. Record the terms and stories used by elders. They add detail that a generic pan-Indian festival summary often misses.

Keep the day comfortable

Include rest after an early morning. Adhere to current local guidelines for any public celebrations and opt for quieter activities when suitable. Phone calls to relatives, a shared breakfast, and a family photo can ground the day without a tiring schedule.
