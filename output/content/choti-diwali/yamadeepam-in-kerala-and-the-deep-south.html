Yamadeepam in Kerala and the Deep South

Lamp offerings associated with Yama and ancestors are present in various Indian traditions, yet they do not constitute a single, uniform Choti Diwali practice across Kerala.

Article 27 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Regional traditions/Article 27

Lamp offerings associated with Yama and ancestors are present in various Indian traditions, yet they do not constitute a single, uniform Choti Diwali practice across Kerala. Kerala's Diwali participation itself varies between communities and households. A careful account leaves space for that diversity.

Ask which tradition is being described

The term Yamadeepam can denote a family lamp ritual, a temple ceremony, or a wider devotional concept. It is important to specify the community, time, and intent before adopting its procedures. Customs from one region should not be generalized and applied to another simply under a southern umbrella.

Keep remembrance simple

A family may illuminate a supervised lamp in their regular prayer area, honor a relative, and share a story. Adhere to customary household traditions for offerings and prayers. Newcomers can observe respectfully without performing specialized rituals.

Avoid blending separate lamp festivals

Kerala and nearby areas observe other significant lamp-related festivals at various times throughout their calendars. A similarity in appearance does not mean they are variations of Naraka Chaturdashi. The names, seasonal context, and local interpretations are important.

Make the article useful at home

Record who upholds the tradition and what significance it holds for them. If your household does not observe such a custom, a thoughtful conversation about ancestral memory suffices; there is no requirement to adopt a ritual simply because it is listed in a general festival guide.
