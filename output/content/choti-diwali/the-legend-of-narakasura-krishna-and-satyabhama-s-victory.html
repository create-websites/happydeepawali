The Legend of Narakasura: Krishna and Satyabhama's Victory

Article 2 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Stories & significance/Article 2

The Narakasura tale provides Choti Diwali with a central theme: power must be held accountable to justice. In common retellings, Krishna faces the king of Pragjyotishpura, accompanied by Satyabhama. The story concludes with prisoners being freed and a community rising from fear.

Read the story as a living tradition

Puranic narration, regional performance and family storytelling do not always assign the same role to every character. Some popular retellings place Satyabhama at the decisive moment of Narakasura's defeat; others emphasize Krishna. Introduce the version you are telling rather than presenting a single account as the only authentic one.

Put the freed women at the centre

The story is frequently condensed into a battle and the capture of many women, commonly cited as 16,000 or 16,100. The ethical focus lies in freeing them from oppression and reclaiming their dignity. Do not depict their suffering merely as an ornamental element in a tale of heroism.

Tell it to children thoughtfully

Start with the issue of a person abusing their power, highlight the bravery needed to confront unfairness, and conclude with the idea of freedom. Avoid graphic violence. Encourage children to draw a lamp symbolizing kindness or to write a sentence about defending someone who is left out.

Connect story and celebration

A diya can symbolize the dispelling of fear and the duty to wield strength wisely. This understanding creates a meaningful connection between mythology, daily behavior, and the celebratory household.
