Faraal Preparation in Maharashtra

Article 28 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Regional traditions/Article 28

Maharashtrian Diwali faraal includes a variety of sweets and savories like chakli, shankarpali, karanji, and laddoos. The preparations usually start before Naraka Chaturdashi. Choti Diwali often serves as the last day for arranging everything, rather than the day when all items are freshly made.

Plan by task and storage needs

Identify the items your family truly likes, then allocate mixing, shaping, cooking, and packing throughout the time you have. Prepare smaller portions if your household is small. Buying one challenging ingredient can free up time for a recipe that holds special significance for your family.

Create a practical work area

Keep raw ingredients, finished dishes, and packaging materials apart. Allow children to assist with safe tasks like shaping or labeling, while adults manage hot oil. Avoid juggling multiple intensive cooking tasks simultaneously just to finish an extensive menu.

Offer a balanced faraal plate

Offer a sweet, a savory, and a drink instead of overloading each plate. When sharing with those outside your household, note ingredients like nuts, sesame, dairy, or wheat. Allow guests to select freely.

Record the details that matter

Family recipes frequently rely more on texture and look than exact cup measurements. Pay attention to these indicators and take a photo during the shaping process. This creates a helpful reference for the following year, rather than just an image of the completed dish.
