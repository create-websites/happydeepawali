The Midnight Transition: Preparing for Badi Diwali

Getting ready for the main Diwali festivities doesn’t mean staying up until midnight. Choti Diwali provides a good opportunity to complete some tasks and verify the…

Article 49 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Food, hosting & celebration/Article 49

Preparing for the main Diwali celebration does not require staying awake until midnight. Choti Diwali is a useful time to finish a few tasks, confirm the next day's plan and leave the household rested. The actual worship time should come from a local calendar where relevant.

Finish the tasks that prevent a rush

Verify the lamp supply, prepare a clean altar cloth, and gather non-perishable puja items in one place. Go over the menu and serving dishes. Lay out clothes and confirm the expected arrival times of guests or relatives.

Handle food according to the recipe

Certain ingredients might require soaking or other prior preparation, though not all recipes gain from an overnight process. Use a reliable recipe and proper storage guidelines. Mark containers clearly so another family member can assist without uncertainty.

Close the house thoughtfully

Store tools and packaging properly, ensure exits remain unobstructed, and extinguish all flames before everyone goes to sleep. An akhand-jyot requires dedicated, continuous monitoring and must never serve as a justification for leaving a regular lamp unattended overnight.

Leave space for the next day

Create a brief morning checklist focusing solely on the essentials. A family story or a gentle greeting can signal the change without any late-night rush. Being prepared means having the energy to greet others and join in the celebration.
