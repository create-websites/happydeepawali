Traditional Brass vs. Clay Diyas: What to Use?

Both clay and brass diyas are fitting for a Choti Diwali home. Clay provides a traditional earthy feel, while brass may hold the warmth of years of family tradition.

Article 35 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Lamps & home preparation/Article 35

Both clay and brass diyas are fitting for a Choti Diwali home. Clay provides a traditional earthy feel, while brass may hold the warmth of years of family tradition. The appropriate choice depends on the household's ritual practice, the condition of the lamp and where it will be placed.

Compare use rather than status

A small clay diya is simple to gather in a group. A brass lamp might be practical for a dedicated prayer area. The material does not determine the sincerity of the offering, and a heavy lamp isn’t necessarily stable on an inappropriate surface.

Check each lamp first

Use intact lamps free of cracks, significant damage, or loose components. Commercial clay diyas are typically kiln-fired; do not rely on an unbaked homemade vessel to securely contain oil. Always set each oil lamp on a heat-resistant tray to safeguard the surface beneath.

Follow household requirements precisely

Certain rituals may require a designated lamp or wick setup. Inquire about that particular practice instead of accepting a general statement that brass is prohibited or clay must be thrown away after a single use.

Plan reuse and clean-up

Let lamps cool fully before handling or cleaning them. Keep functional lamps safely for use in the next festival. The environmental impact of a choice is influenced not only by its label as traditional but also by how it is produced, utilized, and reused.
