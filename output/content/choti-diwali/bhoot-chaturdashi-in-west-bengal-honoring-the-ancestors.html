Bhoot Chaturdashi in West Bengal: Honoring the Ancestors

Bhoot Chaturdashi imparts a unique Bengali character to the Diwali festivities. The celebration is defined by lamps, ancestral memories, and family tales.

Article 23 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Regional traditions/Article 23

Bhoot Chaturdashi imparts a unique Bengali character to the Diwali festivities. The celebration is defined by lamps, ancestral memories, and family tales. It is often described through fourteen diyas and fourteen kinds of leafy greens, although explanations and practices differ among families.

Begin with remembrance

Some families connect the lamps to fourteen generations of forebears. Others highlight themes of protection, dispelling darkness, or inviting remembered ancestors through light. Present these as family interpretations without insisting on a single agreed-upon origin story.

Arrange a gentle evening

Arrange clean lamp spots during daylight hours. Invite an elder to share a brief story about a person unknown to the children. A name, an old photo, or a recalled favorite dish can make ancestry feel intimate instead of distant.

Give folklore its proper place

Tales of spirits can contribute to the ambiance, yet the celebration should not scare children. Clarify that families share stories and light lamps to symbolize connection, tradition, and affection. Refrain from stating supernatural beliefs as proven truths.

Keep related festivals distinct

Bhoot Chaturdashi falls close to Kali Puja in the Bengali calendar, yet the two are distinct rituals and not alternative names for the same observance. Refer to a local panjika for the correct timing and consult the household about the specific traditions they follow.
