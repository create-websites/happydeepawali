Capturing the Festival: Photography Tips for Diya Lighting

Diya photographs are most effective when they capture the warmth of the scene without requiring people to put themselves in danger near the flames.

Article 47 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Food, hosting & celebration/Article 47

Diya photographs are most effective when they capture the warmth of the scene without requiring people to put themselves in danger near the flames. Choti Diwali offers close details, portraits and household stories. Choose the picture you want before the lamps are lit.

Begin with a stable composition

Remove any visual distractions and select an angle that places the lamp on a stable surface. Elements like a tray, hands arranging unlit diyas, or an entrance feature can help convey the scene. Ensure children stay at a safe distance and refrain from relocating a lit lamp just to enhance the background.

Protect the flame detail

On a phone, tap close to the brightest spot and lower the exposure if the flame appears completely washed out. Try several shots since camera apps differ. A compatible camera performs better in low light; portrait subjects should remain relaxed and avoid stiff poses.

Use surrounding light thoughtfully

A minimal level of ambient light can illuminate faces while maintaining a cozy ambiance. Do not position fabric or makeshift diffusers close to an open flame. Battery-powered lamps are handy when setting up a staged portrait that requires multiple tweaks.

Capture people as well as objects

With permission, capture the preparation, a communal greeting, and the completed meal. Take a few shots, then set the camera aside. The evening should be an enjoyable experience for everyone, not an extended photo session disrupted by worship.
