Eco-Friendly Lighting Solutions for Choti Diwali

A sustainable Choti Diwali decoration starts with items the household already has. It involves reusing lamps, selecting a few carefully chosen lights, and reducing the duration they are switched on…

Article 39 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Lamps & home preparation/Article 39

A sustainable Choti Diwali decoration starts with what the household already has. Using reused lamps, a select few carefully chosen lights, and a reduced operating time can produce a cozy atmosphere without needing to purchase an entirely new set every year.

Give existing decorations a second use

Clean usable diya holders, inspect reusable decorations, and properly fix what can be mended. Borrow a tray or share a set of lights with family members. A product is not environmentally better just because its packaging features festive or eco-friendly wording.

Combine light sources thoughtfully

Use supervised oil lamps when the ritual requires them, and opt for battery lamps in places where open flames are unsafe. Reuse rechargeable batteries if they are compatible. For electric light strings, inspect their condition and follow product guidelines instead of connecting multiple sets to create an improvised chain.

Keep the display intentional

Illuminate the entrance and a key interior feature. Employ a timer only if it is appropriate for the device, and turn off decorative lights when they are no longer in use. Solar lights rely on their exposure and maintenance; avoid guaranteeing that all units will function identically.

Plan what happens afterwards

Store decorations in a dry place, retain packaging that safeguards reusable items, and sort electrical waste correctly. Traditional oil, clay, wax, and LEDs all have distinct effects; a thorough guide prevents labeling any one as universally sustainable without evaluating its entire lifecycle.
