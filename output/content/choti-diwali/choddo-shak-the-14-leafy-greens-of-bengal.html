Choddo Shak: The 14 Leafy Greens of Bengal

Choddo Shak, meaning fourteen greens, is a Bengali seasonal culinary tradition linked to Bhoot Chaturdashi. This dish combines cooking with family memories.

Article 24 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Regional traditions/Article 24

Choddo Shak, meaning fourteen greens, is a Bengali seasonal culinary tradition linked to Bhoot Chaturdashi. This dish combines cooking with family memories. The exact selection can vary with locality, market availability and household practice.

Shop with someone who knows the greens

Markets might offer a ready-made selection throughout the season. Request the names and check for freshness instead of assuming every bunch includes the same species. Avoid gathering unknown leaves or relying solely on a photo to identify edible plants.

Prepare a manageable dish

Sort the leaves, remove any damaged parts, and rinse off all the soil completely. Prepare the chosen leaves following a reliable family recipe. A straightforward preparation allows the distinct textures and flavors to be more noticeable than in a heavily seasoned dish.

Allow for availability

Diaspora households might not have access to all fourteen familiar greens. They can prepare a smaller, familiar variety, note the traditional names, and describe the custom during the meal. Counting should never lead to including an unknown plant.

Keep cultural and health claims separate

The meal can symbolize protection or seasonal renewal within a family's beliefs. That does not establish that fourteen greens prevent supernatural harm or cure disease. Serve it as a valued food tradition and enjoy the conversation it makes possible.
