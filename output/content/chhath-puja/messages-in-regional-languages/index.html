CONTEXT & LOCAL PRACTICE

A living Sun festival, rooted in eastern India.

Chhath Puja is particularly important to communities of Bihar, Jharkhand and eastern Uttar Pradesh, including Bhojpuri-, Maithili-, Magahi- and other regional-speaking families. The festival honours Surya (the Sun) and Chhathi Maiya. Many families maintain the tradition elsewhere in India and abroad; this directory addresses participants rather than claiming a universal statewide practice.

The four core observances are Nahay Khay, Kharna, Sandhya Arghya (offerings to the setting sun) and Usha Arghya (offerings to the rising sun). Thekua and other prasad, bamboo baskets and riverbank or community-ghat gatherings are associated with many observances. Chhath is also celebrated in the Chaitra season by some communities. This page does NOT claim October 31, 2026 is Chhath Puja; confirm dates and local sunrise/sunset separately.
