Coconut ladoo recipe

Ingredients and directions from your original page.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup dry coconut powder
  • 1 cup milk powder
  • 3/4 cup sugar powder
  • 5-6 teaspoons boiled milk
  • Raisins or badam for garnish

Directions

  1. Combine coconut powder, milk powder, and sugar powder in a mixing bowl.
  2. Slowly add milk and bind the mixture together.
  3. Shape the mixture into laddoos.
  4. Top each laddoo with a raisin or badam before serving to complete your coconut ladoos.
Festival twist: Include cardamom powder and a couple of saffron strands to achieve the traditional Pongal fragrance.

Coconut ladoo for Sankranti & Pongal

Why coconut ladoo for Sankranti?

While sesame-jaggery sweets are traditional Sankranti treats, coconut ladoo is just as popular for its comforting, nutritious, and convenient qualities at winter get-togethers.

Why coconut ladoo for Pongal?

Pongal is a celebration of the harvest season, during which sweet dishes are offered as prasadam. Coconut plays a prominent role in South Indian cuisine, making coconut ladoo a popular festive treat enjoyed alongside Sakkarai Pongal.

Best time to make

Prepare them either on the morning of the festival or the night before. Allow them to sit for 30–60 minutes to set perfectly.

How to serve

Enjoy after a celebratory feast with a cup of coffee or tea, or gift in a box with a thoughtful card.

Pongal serving ideas

As prasadam

Serve coconut ladoo alongside Sakkarai Pongal or sweet rice for a delicious festive meal.

Gift tray

Combine ladoos, sugarcane, and a greeting card to create a harvest-themed gift.

Kids’ mini ladoos

Make bite-size balls and roll in coconut flakes—easy and cute.

Variations

Fresh coconut version
Substitute dry coconut powder with freshly grated coconut. Toast it lightly for 2–3 minutes to eliminate moisture before continuing.
Condensed milk shortcut
Omit milk powder and sugar. Instead, include ¾ cup desiccated coconut and ½ cup condensed milk. Cook for 2–3 minutes, then cool, shape into balls, and coat.
Vegan option
Mix coconut milk powder (or almond powder) with warm coconut milk and sweeten with powdered jaggery or sugar.
Flavor upgrades
Include cardamom, saffron strands, a touch of nutmeg, or rose petals. Finish by coating in additional coconut flakes to achieve a snowball appearance.

Tips & storage

Binding tip
Gradually pour in the milk to avoid making the mixture too sticky. If it becomes too wet, simply mix in a little more coconut powder.
Texture tip
Lightly roast the coconut to enhance its nutty aroma, being careful not to over-brown it.
Storage
Keep in an airtight container. Store at room temperature for 1-2 days in cool weather or in the fridge for up to 5-7 days. Remember to bring to room temperature before serving.
Gift idea
Take 3-4 ladoos and wrap them in butter paper, then put them in a small box and include a Sankranti/Pongal message

FAQ

Can I use jaggery instead of sugar?
Sure. For optimal binding, it is recommended to use finely powdered jaggery. If the jaggery is moist, it is advised to dry it slightly (by spreading it for 10 minutes) before incorporating it
Why are my ladoos not holding shape?
If the mixture is too dry, add a teaspoon of warm milk; if too crumbly, knead for 1–2 minutes to activate binding.
