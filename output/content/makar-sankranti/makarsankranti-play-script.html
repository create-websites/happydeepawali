The Journey of the Sun

A School Play on Makar Sankranti, Uttarayan, Bihu & Pongal

10–15 Minutes School Assembly
Ready-to-use script for cultural programs.

Cast List

  • Narrator (1–2 students)
  • Surya (Sun God)
  • Sage
  • Farmers (1 & 2)
  • Child
  • Villagers (Group)
  • Assamese Farmer
  • Tamil Farmer
  • Lord Krishna (Optional)
  • Chorus / Dancers
Tip: Have Surya wear bright yellow/orange costumes and dress the farmers in traditional regional attire.

Scene 1: The Sun and The Seasons

Soft music playing. Surya stands center stage with glowing light.
Narrator: In ancient times, when humans were deeply connected to nature, the Sun represented more than just a source of light in the sky. Surya, the giver of life, warmth, and time itself.
Surya: Months of traveling south have brought me to a cold world. But now, it is time to turn northward and begin a new journey.
(Music rises)

Scene 2: Makar Sankranti – The Sacred Turn

Sage: Today, the Sun enters Makara Rashi. This holy day is called Makar Sankranti!
Narrator: It is thought that light conquers darkness today, and performing good deeds brings abundant blessings.
(Villagers enter with folded hands)
Villagers: (Together) We thank you, Surya Dev!
Child: (Offering sweets) Use sesame and jaggery to make our lives sweet and our words kind.
Surya: Let harmony return to families. Let forgiveness heal old wounds.
(Short pause)
Narrator: On this day, Surya even visits his son Shani, teaching the world the power of reconciliation.

Scene 3: Uttarayan – Hopes Take Flight

Sound of wind. Children pretend to fly kites.
Narrator: With Makar Sankranti begins Uttarayan — the Sun’s northward journey.
Farmer 1: The days are getting longer!
Farmer 2: The cold is fading. Hope has returned!
Child: Look! Our kites touch the sky!
Narrator: As kites soar above, they carry with them dreams, gratitude, and joy, symbolizing the embrace of health, warmth, and fresh starts by people below.

Scene 4: Magh Bihu – Fire of Gratitude (Assam)

Bonfire prop or symbolic flame on stage.
Narrator: The Sun travels east to the fertile lands of Assam.
Assamese Farmer: Our harvest is complete. Let us thank fire and nature.
(Villagers circle the bonfire)
All: (Softly) May hardship burn away. May abundance stay.
Narrator: This is Magh Bihu — a festival honoring the bountiful harvest, togetherness, and the act of sharing food with

Scene 5: Pongal – When Life Overflows (Tamil Nadu)

Clay pot prop. Boiling sound effect.
Tamil Farmer: Milk rises! Rice cooks!
All: (Joyfully) Pongalo Pongal!
Narrator: Pongal means to overflow — overflowing with prosperity, happiness, and blessings.
(Lord Krishna enters)
Krishna: Instead of worshipping power alone, show reverence for the Earth, the livestock, and nature, as they provide sustenance for life.
Narrator: On Mattu PongalWe express gratitude and celebrate cows, serving as a reminder to honor all living creatures.

Final Scene: One Sun, One Message

(All characters come together on stage)
Surya: Despite the various ways you celebrate me, your message remains singular.
All Together:

Gratitude to the Sun

Respect for Nature

Sharing with Humanity

Narrator: Makar Sankranti. Uttarayan. Bihu. Pongal. Different names. Different customs. But one eternal story — the Sun's journey and India's unity.
(Music swells)

🌟 The Message

Living in harmony with nature, respecting one another, and sharing with open hearts always brings warmth and abundance back into our lives.
