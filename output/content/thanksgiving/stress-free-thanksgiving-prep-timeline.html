A Stress-Free Thanksgiving Prep Timeline

Follow a two-week Thanksgiving schedule to organize menu planning, shopping, thawing, preparing make-ahead dishes, setting up the table, and ensuring a smoother final cooking day.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Two weeks out: lock the shape of the day

Please verify the number of guests, any dietary restrictions, and the preferred serving time. Select a menu that can be easily prepared with the appliances you have at home, rather than a menu intended for a professional kitchen.

Assign tasks now. Determine which items guests should bring, what can be bought, and what deserves homemade attention.

One week out: shop and prep the environment

Purchase shelf-stable items, drinks, serving essentials, and decorations. Ensure you have roasting pans, thermometers, carving utensils, and storage bins. Make room in the fridge before your last shopping trip.

If you are using a frozen turkey, be sure to plan ahead and calculate the necessary thawing time. According to USDA guidelines, allow approximately one day of refrigerator thawing for every four to five pounds of turkey.

Two to three days out: make what improves with time

Get ready to make cranberry sauce, pie crust, assorted desserts, salad dressings, and bread cubes for stuffing. If your recipe permits, chop up some hearty vegetables. And if your dining area isn't being used for regular meals, go ahead and set the table.

Thanksgiving Day: work backward from serving time

Record the times when dishes enter and exit the oven, as well as their resting times. Start by cooking dishes that can be kept warm longer, reserving fragile items for later. Allow extra time before serving the meal to avoid carving and preparing gravy while guests are seated.

Frequently asked questions

What should I make first?

Begin with dishes that have a longer shelf life, such as cranberry sauce, desserts, bread drying, and certain casseroles. Save the preparation of crisp vegetables and the final cooking of the turkey for a later time.

How much buffer time should I allow?

Allowing for a minimum of 30 to 60 minutes of free time prior to serving is essential in order to account for potential delays in slow cooking, reheating, and carving.
