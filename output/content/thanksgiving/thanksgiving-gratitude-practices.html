Thanksgiving Gratitude Practices Beyond “What Are You Thankful For?”

Enhance the depth of Thanksgiving gratitude by utilizing story prompts, handwritten notes, intergenerational discussions, and mindful practices that steer clear of artificial positivity.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingGratitude, Wellness & Traditions

Move from lists to stories

Instead of one-word answers, can you share a story with me? Who offered you support this year? What everyday moment do you want to cherish? What valuable lesson did you gain from someone at this gathering?

Make room for complicated years

Gratitude doesn't mean ignoring the difficult parts of life. Families can recognize challenges like grief, illness, job changes, and conflict while also appreciating the support, beauty, and moments of stability. This approach to gratitude is more genuine and less about putting on a show.

Use written gratitude for quieter guests

Use place cards as note cards for guests to express appreciation, share memories, or jot down their hopes for the future. Children can also participate by drawing instead of writing.

Turn gratitude into action

An effective final question to ask is: How can we channel our gratitude into action? This could involve reaching out to someone in need, contributing to a local charity, preserving a cherished family dish, or making time for loved ones before time slips away.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make gratitude activities less awkward?

Make story prompts optional, concise, and specific. They tend to flow more naturally compared to mandatory formal speeches.

Is gratitude appropriate during a hard year?

Gratitude and sorrow can coexist if one does not turn away from adversity.
