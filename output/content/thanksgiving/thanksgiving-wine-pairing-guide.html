Thanksgiving Wine Pairings: A Flexible Guide

Pair the sweet, savory, and herbal flavors of Thanksgiving with a variety of red, white, and sparkling wine options as well as nonalcoholic alternatives to accommodate all guests.

Read the guideAll 20 articles

Why Thanksgiving is a pairing challenge

On Thanksgiving, a plate full of roasted poultry, herbs, butter, tart cranberry, sweet squash, gravy, and dessert awaits. Rather than seeking out a single "perfect" bottle, opt for wines that boast good acidity, moderate alcohol, and plenty of fruit to complement the array of flavors.

White and sparkling styles

White wines like Riesling, Chenin Blanc, Pinot Gris, and lightly oaked Chardonnay, with their fresh acidity, are excellent choices to balance rich sides. Sparkling wine, with its bubbles and acidity, can also be versatile in refreshing the palate.

Red styles that stay nimble

Red wines that are lighter to medium-bodied tend to pair better with turkey compared to heavily tannic wines. Varieties such as Pinot Noir, Gamay, and certain Grenache-based wines can enhance the flavors of herbs and roasted dishes without being too overpowering.

Dessert and nonalcoholic options

When enjoying pumpkin or apple desserts, opt for a wine that matches the sweetness level of the dessert rather than a dry table wine. Nonalcoholic options like sparkling cider, tea, grape-based drinks, and botanical sodas can also be paired successfully by balancing sweetness, acidity, and spice.

Note: Alcohol is not required. Please serve responsibly and offer equally considerate non-alcoholic alternatives.

Frequently asked questions

What wine style is easiest with Thanksgiving?

Vibrant, food-friendly wines with balanced acidity are known for their versatility. Sparkling wine, Riesling, Pinot Noir, and Gamay are popular choices.

Should every guest drink wine?

Make sure to always provide attractive non-alcoholic choices and water. Pairing food with drinks is meant to enhance the dining experience, not force anyone to consume alcohol.
