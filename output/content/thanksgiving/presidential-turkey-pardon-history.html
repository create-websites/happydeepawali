The Presidential Turkey Pardon: History, Myth and Media Ritual

Discover the history of the White House turkey pardon, distinguishing between its traditional roots and its modern-day media spectacle.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHistory, Heritage & Perspectives

The origin stories are messier than the joke

Tales of presidents saving turkeys date back long before the current ceremony, with not all being official Thanksgiving "pardons." The White House Historical Association recalls a tale of Abraham Lincoln's son Tad convincing him to spare a Christmas turkey, and subsequent presidents occasionally letting presentation birds survive.

These episodes should be viewed more as predecessors rather than a single cohesive formal tradition.

When the modern pardon took shape

Turkey presentations to presidents were a common occurrence in the twentieth century, with John F. Kennedy famously sparing a turkey in 1963 and Ronald Reagan employing pardon language in the 1980s.

The tradition is said to have begun in 1989, when President George H. W. Bush gave a turkey a presidential pardon. Subsequent presidents have kept the lighthearted pre-Thanksgiving custom alive.

A ritual of media, agriculture and humor

The event combines various American traditions, including the presidency, agricultural industry associations, festive TV specials, and political satire. Speeches often feature humor and witty banter, with the turkey serving as a lighthearted prop in the midst of a hectic political climate.

The "pardon" is more of a symbolic gesture than a legal act of clemency, making it a ceremonial rather than a constitutional exercise.

How to discuss the tradition neutrally

The event, rooted in White House tradition and evolving presidential customs, can be examined without being seen as significant statecraft or trivial spectacle. It is a recurring media tradition that satisfies the public's appetite for seasonal rituals.

Frequently asked questions

Which president made the turkey pardon an annual tradition?

The contemporary yearly custom is typically attributed to President George H. W. Bush in 1989.

Is a turkey pardon a real legal pardon?

No, it is a symbolic ritual, not a pardon for a criminal act based on the constitution.
