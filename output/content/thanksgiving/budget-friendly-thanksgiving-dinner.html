Budget-Friendly Thanksgiving Dinner Planning

Organize a budget-friendly Thanksgiving feast by creating a thoughtful menu, using seasonal ingredients and store-brand items, encouraging potluck contributions, and minimizing waste.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Set a per-person target before shopping

Before heading to the store, determine your budget by dividing the total amount you're willing to spend by the number of guests, to help make decisions on what to prioritize and what to compromise on when planning your holiday meals.

Choose one centerpiece, not five

Opt for a plant-based centerpiece or turkey as the main dish to focus on one protein. Pair it with affordable seasonal sides like potatoes, squash, carrots, rolls, beans, and easy salads to create a satisfying meal.

Buy ingredients that work in several dishes

Onions, celery, herbs, stock, flour, and butter or oil are versatile ingredients that can enhance stuffing, gravy, vegetables, and casseroles, helping to reduce waste from half-used packages and forgotten specialty items.

Use potluck logic strategically

Assign categories for guests to contribute, such as dessert, salad, vegetable side, drinks, or bread, to distribute cost and labor and avoid duplicating casseroles.

Include leftovers in the budget as well. By utilizing containers, making sandwiches, soup, and next-day meals, you can stretch the holiday grocery bill into multiple days of meals instead of just one large dinner.

Frequently asked questions

Is a whole turkey always the cheapest main?

Sometimes it's best to compare local prices and guest count to determine the most budget-friendly option, whether that be a smaller turkey, turkey breast, plant-based centerpiece, or potluck main dish.

How do I avoid duplicate potluck dishes?

Categorize items broadly and maintain a shared list to ensure guests can easily identify what has been accounted for.
