The Friendsgiving Hosting Playbook

Organize a chill Friendsgiving with potluck sign-ups, dietary planning, drink and dessert stations, seating arrangements, and easy cleanup strategies.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Decide what Friendsgiving means for your group

Specify the format of your Friendsgiving gathering in the invitation, whether it's a formal dinner, buffet, brunch, or casual potluck, so guests know what to bring - a finished dish, ingredients, drinks, or just themselves.

Use categories instead of micromanaging recipes

Encourage variety at the potluck by assigning categories like mains, vegetables, starches, salads, desserts, and drinks instead of specific dishes. This allows for creativity while ensuring a balanced meal.

Inquire about allergies and dietary needs prior to task assignments. Clearly mark dishes at the table to avoid guests probing each cook.

Create stations that reduce kitchen traffic

Place beverages at a distance from the cooking zone. Designate a separate area for desserts or coffee to be served post-dinner. Arrange plates and utensils at the start of the buffet line and napkins at the opposite ends.

Make cleanup part of the design

Have empty food containers on hand for leftovers and designate specific areas for trash, recycling, and dirty dishes. If the group is cooperative, have two or three people help with a quick 15-minute cleanup to avoid leaving all the work to the host.

Frequently asked questions

Should the host cook the turkey?

Friendsgiving is most successful when the host offers to provide one main dish and guests contribute to the rest of the menu, but only if the host is willing.

How do I handle dietary restrictions?

Gather them in advance, clearly mark dishes, and make sure each guest has multiple substantial choices.
