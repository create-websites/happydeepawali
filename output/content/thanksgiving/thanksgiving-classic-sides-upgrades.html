Upgrading Classic Thanksgiving Sides Without Losing Tradition

Elevate classic Thanksgiving dishes like stuffing, mashed potatoes, cranberry sauce, and green bean casserole with modern and delicious twists while still maintaining that traditional holiday essence.

Read the guideAll 20 articles

Keep the memory, change the technique

Holiday side dishes are expected to be traditional, but updating them with a few intentional upgrades can keep them fresh without straying too far from the classics. Adding too many changes at once can make the meal feel more like a fancy restaurant tasting menu than a cozy Thanksgiving spread.

Stuffing: build layers of savory flavor

Ensure the bread is toasted well to maintain its texture. Cook onions and celery until caramelized, then incorporate herbs like sage, thyme, or parsley. To add variety, think about including roasted mushrooms, apples, chestnuts, or toasted nuts. Maintain a balanced ratio of add-ins to complement the bread and herb flavors.

Mashed potatoes: texture first

Opt for starchy potatoes for a fluffy mash, pat them dry post-boiling, and heat butter and dairy before combining. Be cautious of overmixing to prevent a gummy texture. Enhance with brown butter, roasted garlic, or a hint of horseradish for added flavor complexity.

Cranberry sauce and green beans

Cranberry sauce pairs well with acidity and spice: adding orange zest, ginger, or a hint of warm spice can enhance the fruit. To freshen up a green bean casserole, swap out canned ingredients for blanched beans, sautéed mushrooms, and crispy onions while maintaining the dish's flavor profile.

Frequently asked questions

How many side dishes should I make?

Adjust the amount of substantial sides, bread, and cranberry sauce on a mixed table based on the number of guests and the size of your kitchen.

Can sides be made ahead?

Several dishes can be prepared in advance, such as cranberry sauce, casseroles, and stuffing components, while mashed potatoes should be reheated with extra attention.
