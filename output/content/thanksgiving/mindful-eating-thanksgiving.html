Mindful Eating at the Thanksgiving Table

Savor the Thanksgiving feast at a leisurely pace, being mindful of hunger cues and portion control, without letting strict diet rules overshadow the festivities.

🍁 ThanksgivingGratitude, Wellness & Traditions

Arrive hungry, not ravenous

Missing meals to reduce calorie intake can lead to difficulty recognizing fullness later on. Having a regular breakfast or a light lunch sets you up to fully savor dinner, rather than feeling rushed to eat.

Survey before serving

Survey the entire selection before making your choices. Opt for the dishes that truly appeal to you, especially those with sentimental value, and serve yourself small portions initially. You can always go back for seconds.

Slow the first ten minutes

At the start of a holiday meal, it's tempting to hurry through. Take breaks from eating, engage in conversation, savor the textures and temperatures, and allow the meal to progress naturally. Slowing down your pace can enhance the comfort of the experience.

Avoid moral labels for food

Thanksgiving meals can include a variety of foods without the labels of "good," "bad," "clean," or "cheat." Food can nourish, bring pleasure, reflect culture, and create lasting memories. If you have specific dietary requirements, it's important to follow your clinician's guidance. Otherwise, it's generally more sustainable to approach the holiday with flexibility rather than guilt.

Frequently asked questions

Should I skip breakfast before Thanksgiving dinner?

Eating a balanced meal earlier in the day can help people approach dinner without feeling ravenous and uncomfortable.

What if I eat more than planned?

Don't feel the need to punish or compensate for one big meal. Just get back to your regular routine and prioritize comfort, hydration, and wellbeing.
