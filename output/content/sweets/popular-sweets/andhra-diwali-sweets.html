12 Traditional Andhra Diwali Sweets

Andhra's Deepavali sweets mix jaggery-rich home classics with famous town specialities.

By Prashant Dhingra

See all 12 sweets Keeping guide

In short

Andhra's Deepavali sweets mix jaggery-rich home classics with famous town specialities. Homes make ariselu, sunnundalu, bellam gavvalu, boorelu and bobbatlu; Kakinada kaja, Atreyapuram pootharekulu and Machilipatnam's bandar laddu are bought or gifted. Many of the fried and jaggery sweets also return at Sankranti.

Diwali sweets in Andhra Pradesh

Andhra sweets lean on jaggery, rice flour, urad dal and ghee. The East Godavari district alone gives two of the best-known: the paper-thin pootharekulu of Atreyapuram and the juicy kaja of Kakinada. Long-keeping sweets such as ariselu and sunnundalu are made in bulk and shared across the season.

The 12 sweets

What each one is, what it is made of, and how long it keeps.

  1. 01 of 12

    Ariselu

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    Rice-flour and jaggery discs topped with sesame, fried and pressed between two ladles to squeeze out excess oil. Andhra's cousin of anarsa and adhirasam.

    Made with: Soaked rice flour, jaggery, sesame seeds, ghee

  2. 02 of 12

    Pootharekulu

    Layered specialtyKeeps 2+ weeks

    'Paper sweet' from Atreyapuram: tissue-thin rice starch sheets layered with ghee and sugar or jaggery, folded into rolls. Making the sheets is a specialist craft.

    Made with: Rice starch sheets, ghee, powdered sugar or jaggery, nuts

  3. 03 of 12

    Kakinada Kaja

    Syrup-soakedKeeps about a week

    A layered fried pastry from Kakinada, soaked so the inside stays juicy with syrup while the outside is crisp.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, sugar syrup

  4. 04 of 12

    Bandar Laddu

    LadooKeeps 2+ weeks

    A rich, coarse-textured laddu from Machilipatnam (Bandar), made with gram flour pounded with jaggery and plenty of ghee.

    Made with: Gram flour, jaggery or sugar, ghee, cardamom

  5. 05 of 12

    Sunnundalu

    LadooKeeps 2+ weeks

    Ladoos of roasted and ground urad dal with jaggery and ghee. Protein-rich and long-keeping; often made for new mothers too.

    Made with: Roasted urad dal, jaggery or sugar, ghee

  6. 06 of 12

    Boorelu (Poornalu)

    Fried & crispBest eaten fresh

    Balls of sweet chana dal and coconut filling dipped in rice and urad dal batter and deep-fried. Crisp outside, soft inside; best eaten warm.

    Made with: Chana dal, jaggery, coconut, rice and urad dal batter

  7. 07 of 12

    Bobbatlu

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Andhra's puran poli: a thin flatbread stuffed with sweet chana dal and jaggery, roasted with ghee.

    Made with: Chana dal, jaggery, maida, ghee

  8. 08 of 12

    Bellam Gavvalu

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    Small shell-shaped pieces (gavvalu means cowrie shells) rolled on a fork or comb, fried and coated in thick jaggery syrup.

    Made with: Wheat flour or maida, jaggery syrup

  9. 09 of 12

    Madatha Kaja

    Syrup-soakedKeeps 2+ weeks

    A folded, many-layered kaja (madata means fold), fried crisp and coated in syrup that sets. Drier than Kakinada kaja and keeps longer.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, sugar syrup

  10. 10 of 12

    Rava Laddu

    LadooKeeps about a week

    Roasted semolina and coconut ladoo with cashews and raisins, quick to make on the festival morning.

    Made with: Semolina, coconut, sugar, ghee, cashews

  11. 11 of 12

    Palakova

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Milk slowly reduced to a grainy, caramel-tinged kova with a little sugar. Simple ingredients; the skill is in the stirring.

    Made with: Milk, sugar

  12. 12 of 12

    Kajjikayalu

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadKeeps 2+ weeks

    Andhra's karanji: a crimped crescent pastry with a dry coconut or semolina filling, fried until crisp.

    Made with: Maida, dry coconut or rava, sugar, cardamom

At a glance: type and keeping time

Make the long-keeping sweets first and the milk-based ones last. Keeping times are typical for homemade sweets in an airtight container in a cool kitchen; shop-bought sweets may differ.

Related sweets in other states

  • Ariselu: soaked rice flour and jaggery dough, rested, then fried. Related: Anarsa (Maharashtra), Adhirasam (Tamil Nadu), Anarsa (Bihar).
  • Kajjikayalu: a sealed half-moon pastry with a dry sweet filling, fried crisp. Related: Karanji (Maharashtra), Karjikai (Karnataka), Ghughra (Gujarat), Somas (Tamil Nadu), Pedakiya (Bihar).
  • Bobbatlu: chana dal and jaggery filling inside a thin flatbread. Related: Puran Poli (Maharashtra), Obbattu / Holige (Karnataka).
  • Kakinada Kaja / Madatha Kaja: many thin layers fried crisp, then sugared or soaked. Related: Chirote (Maharashtra), Chiroti (Karnataka), Khaja (Bihar).
  • Palakova: milk cooked down for hours into kova, peda or kunda. Related: Palkova (Tamil Nadu), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Maharashtra), Basundi (Gujarat).

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular Andhra Diwali sweets?

Andhra's Deepavali sweets mix jaggery-rich home classics with famous town specialities. Homes make ariselu, sunnundalu, bellam gavvalu, boorelu and bobbatlu; Kakinada kaja, Atreyapuram pootharekulu and Machilipatnam's bandar laddu are bought or gifted. Many of the fried and jaggery sweets also return at Sankranti.

Which Andhra Diwali sweets keep longest for gifting?

Ariselu, Pootharekulu, Bandar Laddu, Sunnundalu, Bellam Gavvalu, Madatha Kaja and Kajjikayalu keep for two weeks or more in an airtight container, so they can be made first and are the safest to gift or courier.

Which Andhra Diwali sweets need to be made last or refrigerated?

Boorelu (Poornalu), Bobbatlu and Palakova are milk-, chhena-, curd- or fresh-coconut based or best served warm. Make them a day or two ahead at most, refrigerate, and serve within a few days.

Which other states make similar sweets?

Rice and jaggery fried discs: Anarsa (Maharashtra), Adhirasam (Tamil Nadu), Anarsa (Bihar). Crimped crescent pastries: Karanji (Maharashtra), Karjikai (Karnataka), Ghughra (Gujarat), Somas (Tamil Nadu), Pedakiya (Bihar). Sweet-stuffed flatbreads: Puran Poli (Maharashtra), Obbattu / Holige (Karnataka). Layered fried pastries: Chirote (Maharashtra), Chiroti (Karnataka), Khaja (Bihar). Slow-reduced milk sweets: Palkova (Tamil Nadu), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Maharashtra), Basundi (Gujarat).

More Diwali sweets

  • West Bengal 12 Types of Bengali Diwali Sweets Bengali sweets are built on chhena, fresh curdled-milk cheese, kneaded and either simmered in syrup (rasgulla, rajbhog, cham cham), fried (pantua, langcha) or gently cooked (sandesh).
  • Gujarat 12 Traditional Gujarati Diwali Sweets Gujarati Diwali sweets are rich in ghee and gram flour: mohanthal, magaj and sukhdi are made at home in big batches, alongside ghughra, kopra pak and the winter-warming adadiya pak.
  • Karnataka 12 Traditional Karnataka Diwali Sweets Karnataka's Deepavali sweets range from palace classics to town specialities: Mysore pak, Dharwad peda and Belagavi kunda sit beside home favorites such as obbattu (holige), chiroti, karjikai and kobbari burfi.
  • Rajasthan 12 Traditional Rajasthani Diwali Sweets Rajasthani Diwali sweets are rich with ghee, mawa and gram flour: ghevar and rabri ghevar, Jodhpur's mawa kachori, mohanthal, besan chakki, churma ladoo and mishri mawa.
  • Bihar 12 Traditional Bihari Diwali Sweets Bihari festival sweets are rustic and long-keeping: thekua, khaja, anarsa, tilkut, lai and khurma sit beside syrup sweets such as balushahi, malpua and kala jamun.
  • Tamil Nadu 12 Traditional Tamil Nadu Diwali Sweets Tamil Deepavali begins before dawn with an oil bath, new clothes and a plate of homemade sweets (bakshanam).
