12 Traditional Rajasthani Diwali Sweets

Rajasthani Diwali sweets are rich with ghee, mawa and gram flour: ghevar and rabri ghevar, Jodhpur's mawa kachori, mohanthal, besan chakki, churma ladoo and mishri mawa.

By Prashant Dhingra

See all 12 sweets Keeping guide

In short

Rajasthani Diwali sweets are rich with ghee, mawa and gram flour: ghevar and rabri ghevar, Jodhpur's mawa kachori, mohanthal, besan chakki, churma ladoo and mishri mawa. Feeni, balushahi, malpua, moong dal halwa and the winter favorite gajak complete the spread.

Diwali sweets in Rajasthan

Rajasthan's desert climate shaped sweets that keep well, built on ghee, gram flour, sesame and jaggery. Families visit with sweet boxes through the five days of Diwali, and halwais in cities like Jaipur and Jodhpur stay open late in the run-up.

The 12 sweets

What each one is, what it is made of, and how long it keeps.

  1. 01 of 12

    Ghevar

    Syrup-soakedKeeps about a week

    A honeycomb disc of batter poured into hot ghee, then soaked in syrup. Most associated with Teej and Raksha Bandhan but sold through the festival season.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, milk, sugar syrup

  2. 02 of 12

    Rabri Ghevar

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Ghevar topped with thick rabri and nuts. Richer and softer, and it needs refrigeration.

    Made with: Ghevar, rabri, saffron, nuts

  3. 03 of 12

    Mawa Kachori

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Jodhpur's sweet kachori: a fried pastry stuffed with mawa and dry fruit, dipped in syrup.

    Made with: Maida, mawa, nuts, sugar syrup

  4. 04 of 12

    Mohanthal

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps 2+ weeks

    Coarse gram flour roasted in ghee with khoya and syrup, set and cut into squares.

    Made with: Coarse gram flour, ghee, khoya, sugar syrup, saffron

  5. 05 of 12

    Besan Chakki

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps 2+ weeks

    A dense, layered gram-flour barfi, often finished with silver leaf. Rajasthan's everyday festival barfi.

    Made with: Gram flour, ghee, sugar syrup, cardamom, silver leaf

  6. 06 of 12

    Churma Ladoo

    LadooKeeps about a week

    Coarse wheat dough fried or baked, crushed, then mixed with ghee and jaggery and shaped into ladoos.

    Made with: Coarse wheat flour, ghee, jaggery or sugar, cardamom, nuts

  7. 07 of 12

    Feeni

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    Fine fried threads of layered pastry, eaten dry or soaked in warm sweetened milk.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, sugar

  8. 08 of 12

    Mishri Mawa

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    A soft mawa sweet sweetened with mishri and finished with silver leaf.

    Made with: Mawa, mishri (rock sugar), cardamom, silver leaf

  9. 09 of 12

    Balushahi

    Syrup-soakedKeeps about a week

    A flaky fried disc soaked in syrup with a slightly crisp coating.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, yogurt, sugar syrup

  10. 10 of 12

    Malpua

    Syrup-soakedBest eaten fresh

    Sweet pancakes fried in ghee and dipped in syrup, often served with rabri.

    Made with: Flour, milk, sugar syrup, fennel, cardamom

  11. 11 of 12

    Moong Dal Halwa

    Halwa & puddingRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Soaked and ground moong dal cooked in ghee for a long time until golden and grainy. A winter and wedding favorite.

    Made with: Moong dal, ghee, sugar, milk, nuts

  12. 12 of 12

    Gajak

    Brittle & crunchyKeeps 2+ weeks

    A crisp, flaky sesame and jaggery sweet, pounded or pulled into thin layers. A winter specialty.

    Made with: Sesame seeds, jaggery or sugar

Related sweets in other states

  • Balushahi: a layered fried dough disc finished in syrup. Related: Balushahi (Bihar), Badusha (Karnataka), Badusha (Tamil Nadu).
  • Feeni: hair-fine fried pastry nests, eaten dry or with milk. Related: Suterfeni (Gujarat).
  • Mohanthal / Besan Chakki: besan roasted in ghee and set with sugar. Related: Mysore Pak (Karnataka), Mysore Pak (Tamil Nadu), Mohanthal (Gujarat), Magaj (Gujarat), Besan Burfi (Burfi).
  • Gajak: winter sweets of sesame bound with jaggery. Related: Tilkut (Bihar), Til-Gud Ladoo (Laddoos).

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular Rajasthani Diwali sweets?

Rajasthani Diwali sweets are rich with ghee, mawa and gram flour: ghevar and rabri ghevar, Jodhpur's mawa kachori, mohanthal, besan chakki, churma ladoo and mishri mawa. Feeni, balushahi, malpua, moong dal halwa and the winter favorite gajak complete the spread.

Which Rajasthani Diwali sweets keep longest for gifting?

Mohanthal, Besan Chakki, Feeni and Gajak keep for two weeks or more in an airtight container, so they can be made first and are the safest to gift or courier.

Which Rajasthani Diwali sweets need to be made last or refrigerated?

Rabri Ghevar, Mawa Kachori, Mishri Mawa, Malpua and Moong Dal Halwa are milk-, chhena-, curd- or fresh-coconut based or best served warm. Make them a day or two ahead at most, refrigerate, and serve within a few days.

Which other states make similar sweets?

Flaky syrup-soaked discs: Balushahi (Bihar), Badusha (Karnataka), Badusha (Tamil Nadu). Fine pastry threads: Suterfeni (Gujarat). Gram flour and ghee fudges: Mysore Pak (Karnataka), Mysore Pak (Tamil Nadu), Mohanthal (Gujarat), Magaj (Gujarat), Besan Burfi (Burfi). Sesame and jaggery: Tilkut (Bihar), Til-Gud Ladoo (Laddoos).
