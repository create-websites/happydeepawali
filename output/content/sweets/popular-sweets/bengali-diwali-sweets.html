12 Types of Bengali Diwali Sweets

Bengali sweets are built on chhena, fresh curdled-milk cheese, kneaded and either simmered in syrup (rasgulla, rajbhog, cham cham), fried (pantua, langcha) or gently cooked (sandesh).

By Prashant Dhingra

See all 12 sweets Keeping guide

In short

Bengali sweets are built on chhena, fresh curdled-milk cheese, kneaded and either simmered in syrup (rasgulla, rajbhog, cham cham), fried (pantua, langcha) or gently cooked (sandesh). In Bengal, Diwali night is Kali Puja, and these sweets fill the plates for guests and offerings. Most are perishable and are bought fresh from the moira's shop.

Diwali sweets in West Bengal

Diwali in Bengal coincides with Kali Puja, and Bhai Phonta (Bhai Dooj) follows two days later, when sisters feed their brothers a plate heaped with sweets. Because chhena sweets spoil quickly, families buy them fresh for each occasion rather than making a store.

The 12 sweets

What each one is, what it is made of, and how long it keeps.

  1. 01 of 12

    Rasgulla

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Soft, spongy chhena balls cooked in light sugar syrup. Kneading the chhena until smooth is what makes them springy.

    Made with: Chhena, sugar syrup

  2. 02 of 12

    Rajbhog

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    A large, saffron-tinted rasgulla with a filling of nuts or khoya; the 'royal' version of rasgulla.

    Made with: Chhena, saffron, sugar syrup, nuts or khoya filling

  3. 03 of 12

    Gulab Jamun

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Khoya dumplings fried slowly until deep brown and soaked in cardamom and rose syrup. Loved across India.

    Made with: Khoya, maida, sugar syrup, cardamom, rose water

  4. 04 of 12

    Pantua

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Bengal's answer to gulab jamun, made with chhena and a little semolina and fried darker. Firmer and less sweet.

    Made with: Chhena, semolina, sugar syrup

  5. 05 of 12

    Langcha

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    An elongated, dark-fried chhena and khoya sweet soaked in syrup, famously associated with Shaktigarh near Bardhaman.

    Made with: Chhena, khoya, flour, sugar syrup

  6. 06 of 12

    Cham Cham

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Oval chhena sweets cooked in syrup, then rolled in coconut or khoya; firmer and denser than rasgulla.

    Made with: Chhena, sugar syrup, desiccated coconut or khoya

  7. 07 of 12

    Pink Cham Cham

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    The colored party version of cham cham, often flavored with rose. Popular on mixed sweet boxes.

    Made with: Chhena, sugar syrup, coconut, rose color

  8. 08 of 12

    Rasmalai

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Flattened chhena discs soaked in thickened saffron and cardamom milk, served chilled.

    Made with: Chhena discs, thickened milk, saffron, cardamom, pistachios

  9. 09 of 12

    Sandesh

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Fresh chhena kneaded with sugar, gently cooked and pressed into molds. In winter it is made with nolen gur (date-palm jaggery).

    Made with: Fresh chhena, sugar or nolen gur

  10. 10 of 12

    Kesar Sandesh

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Saffron-scented sandesh, often molded into flowers or cups for festive boxes.

    Made with: Chhena, sugar, saffron, pistachios

  11. 11 of 12

    Kheer Kadam

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    A small rasgulla wrapped in a layer of sweet khoya and rolled in grated khoya, so it looks like the kadam flower. A sweet within a sweet.

    Made with: Small rasgulla, khoya, sugar

  12. 12 of 12

    Chocolate Sandesh

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    A modern sandesh made with cocoa or chocolate; a favorite with children and in gift boxes.

    Made with: Chhena, sugar, cocoa or chocolate

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular Bengali Diwali sweets?

Bengali sweets are built on chhena, fresh curdled-milk cheese, kneaded and either simmered in syrup (rasgulla, rajbhog, cham cham), fried (pantua, langcha) or gently cooked (sandesh). In Bengal, Diwali night is Kali Puja, and these sweets fill the plates for guests and offerings. Most are perishable and are bought fresh from the moira's shop.

Which Bengali Diwali sweets need to be made last or refrigerated?

Rasgulla, Rajbhog, Gulab Jamun, Pantua, Langcha, Cham Cham, Pink Cham Cham, Rasmalai, Sandesh, Kesar Sandesh, Kheer Kadam and Chocolate Sandesh are milk-, chhena-, curd- or fresh-coconut based or best served warm. Make them a day or two ahead at most, refrigerate, and serve within a few days.

More Diwali sweets

  • Gujarat 12 Traditional Gujarati Diwali Sweets Gujarati Diwali sweets are rich in ghee and gram flour: mohanthal, magaj and sukhdi are made at home in big batches, alongside ghughra, kopra pak and the winter-warming adadiya pak.
  • Karnataka 12 Traditional Karnataka Diwali Sweets Karnataka's Deepavali sweets range from palace classics to town specialities: Mysore pak, Dharwad peda and Belagavi kunda sit beside home favorites such as obbattu (holige), chiroti, karjikai and kobbari burfi.
  • Rajasthan 12 Traditional Rajasthani Diwali Sweets Rajasthani Diwali sweets are rich with ghee, mawa and gram flour: ghevar and rabri ghevar, Jodhpur's mawa kachori, mohanthal, besan chakki, churma ladoo and mishri mawa.
  • Bihar 12 Traditional Bihari Diwali Sweets Bihari festival sweets are rustic and long-keeping: thekua, khaja, anarsa, tilkut, lai and khurma sit beside syrup sweets such as balushahi, malpua and kala jamun.
  • Tamil Nadu 12 Traditional Tamil Nadu Diwali Sweets Tamil Deepavali begins before dawn with an oil bath, new clothes and a plate of homemade sweets (bakshanam).
  • By type 12 Types of Diwali Laddoos Laddoos are the easiest Diwali sweet to make and share: besan, motichoor and boondi ladoos are the classics, rava and coconut the quick ones.
