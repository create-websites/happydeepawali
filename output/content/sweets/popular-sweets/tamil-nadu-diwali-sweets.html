12 Traditional Tamil Nadu Diwali Sweets

Tamil Deepavali begins before dawn with an oil bath, new clothes and a plate of homemade sweets (bakshanam).

By Prashant Dhingra

See all 12 sweets Keeping guide

In short

Tamil Deepavali begins before dawn with an oil bath, new clothes and a plate of homemade sweets (bakshanam). The essentials are adhirasam, Mysore pak, rava ladoo, boondi ladoo and thengai burfi, with jangiri and badusha from the sweet shop. Palkova, Tirunelveli halwa, somas, nei appam and paal kozhukattai complete the list.

Diwali sweets in Tamil Nadu

In Tamil Nadu, Deepavali is celebrated on Naraka Chaturdashi morning, starting with the ganga snanam oil bath. Sweets and savories (bakshanam) are made over the preceding days and shared with neighbors. Tirunelveli is famous for its halwa and Srivilliputhur for its palkova.

The 12 sweets

What each one is, what it is made of, and how long it keeps.

  1. 01 of 12

    Adhirasam

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    Rice flour and jaggery dough, rested and patted into discs, then fried. The essential Deepavali sweet in many Tamil homes.

    Made with: Soaked rice flour, jaggery, cardamom, ghee

  2. 02 of 12

    Mysore Pak

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps 2+ weeks

    Gram flour, sugar and ghee. The Tamil Nadu style is often the soft, ghee-rich version that melts in the mouth.

    Made with: Gram flour, ghee, sugar syrup

  3. 03 of 12

    Jangiri

    Syrup-soakedRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Flower-shaped urad dal spirals, fried and soaked in syrup.

    Made with: Urad dal batter, sugar syrup

  4. 04 of 12

    Badusha

    Syrup-soakedKeeps about a week

    Flaky fried discs soaked in syrup with a glazed finish.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, yogurt, sugar syrup

  5. 05 of 12

    Rava Ladoo

    LadooKeeps about a week

    Roasted semolina and sugar ladoo with ghee-fried cashews and raisins.

    Made with: Semolina, sugar, ghee, cashews, raisins

  6. 06 of 12

    Boondi Ladoo

    LadooKeeps about a week

    Tiny fried gram-flour drops soaked in syrup and pressed into ladoos.

    Made with: Gram flour boondi, sugar syrup, cardamom, cashews

  7. 07 of 12

    Thengai Burfi

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps about a week

    Coconut burfi (thengai means coconut), cooked with sugar until it sets.

    Made with: Fresh coconut, sugar, cardamom

  8. 08 of 12

    Palkova

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Milk slowly reduced to a soft, grainy sweet. Srivilliputhur's is the most famous.

    Made with: Milk, sugar

  9. 09 of 12

    Tirunelveli Halwa

    Halwa & puddingKeeps about a week

    A glossy, dark halwa made from wheat milk extracted from soaked wheat, cooked for hours with sugar and ghee.

    Made with: Wheat milk, sugar, ghee

  10. 10 of 12

    Somas

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadKeeps 2+ weeks

    Tamil Nadu's karanji, with a dry filling of roasted gram, coconut and sugar.

    Made with: Maida, roasted gram or semolina, coconut, sugar

  11. 11 of 12

    Nei Appam

    Fried & crispBest eaten fresh

    Small sweet rice and jaggery fritters cooked in ghee in a paniyaram pan.

    Made with: Rice, jaggery, banana, ghee

  12. 12 of 12

    Paal Kozhukattai

    Milk & chhenaBest eaten fresh

    Small rice-flour dumplings simmered in sweetened milk or coconut milk.

    Made with: Rice flour, milk or coconut milk, jaggery or sugar, cardamom

Related sweets in other states

  • Adhirasam: soaked rice flour and jaggery dough, rested, then fried. Related: Anarsa (Maharashtra), Ariselu (Andhra Pradesh), Anarsa (Bihar).
  • Somas: a sealed half-moon pastry with a dry sweet filling, fried crisp. Related: Karanji (Maharashtra), Kajjikayalu (Andhra Pradesh), Karjikai (Karnataka), Ghughra (Gujarat), Pedakiya (Bihar).
  • Badusha: a layered fried dough disc finished in syrup. Related: Balushahi (Rajasthan), Balushahi (Bihar), Badusha (Karnataka).
  • Mysore Pak: besan roasted in ghee and set with sugar. Related: Mysore Pak (Karnataka), Mohanthal (Gujarat), Mohanthal (Rajasthan), Magaj (Gujarat), Besan Chakki (Rajasthan), Besan Burfi (Burfi).
  • Palkova: milk cooked down for hours into kova, peda or kunda. Related: Palakova (Andhra Pradesh), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Maharashtra), Basundi (Gujarat).

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular Tamil Diwali sweets?

Tamil Deepavali begins before dawn with an oil bath, new clothes and a plate of homemade sweets (bakshanam). The essentials are adhirasam, Mysore pak, rava ladoo, boondi ladoo and thengai burfi, with jangiri and badusha from the sweet shop. Palkova, Tirunelveli halwa, somas, nei appam and paal kozhukattai complete the list.

Which Tamil Diwali sweets keep longest for gifting?

Adhirasam, Mysore Pak and Somas keep for two weeks or more in an airtight container, so they can be made first and are the safest to gift or courier.

Which Tamil Diwali sweets need to be made last or refrigerated?

Jangiri, Palkova, Nei Appam and Paal Kozhukattai are milk-, chhena-, curd- or fresh-coconut based or best served warm. Make them a day or two ahead at most, refrigerate, and serve within a few days.

Which other states make similar sweets?

Rice and jaggery fried discs: Anarsa (Maharashtra), Ariselu (Andhra Pradesh), Anarsa (Bihar). Crimped crescent pastries: Karanji (Maharashtra), Kajjikayalu (Andhra Pradesh), Karjikai (Karnataka), Ghughra (Gujarat), Pedakiya (Bihar). Flaky syrup-soaked discs: Balushahi (Rajasthan), Balushahi (Bihar), Badusha (Karnataka). Gram flour and ghee fudges: Mysore Pak (Karnataka), Mohanthal (Gujarat), Mohanthal (Rajasthan), Magaj (Gujarat), Besan Chakki (Rajasthan), Besan Burfi (Burfi). Slow-reduced milk sweets: Palakova (Andhra Pradesh), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Maharashtra), Basundi (Gujarat).
