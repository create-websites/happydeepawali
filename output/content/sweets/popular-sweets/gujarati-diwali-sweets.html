12 Traditional Gujarati Diwali Sweets

Gujarati Diwali sweets are rich in ghee and gram flour: mohanthal, magaj and sukhdi are made at home in big batches, alongside ghughra, kopra pak and the winter-warming adadiya pak.

By Prashant Dhingra

See all 12 sweets Keeping guide

In short

Gujarati Diwali sweets are rich in ghee and gram flour: mohanthal, magaj and sukhdi are made at home in big batches, alongside ghughra, kopra pak and the winter-warming adadiya pak. Suterfeni, basundi and shrikhand round out the festive table. Lapsi and kansar are cooked for auspicious starts, including Bestu Varas, the Gujarati New Year the day after Diwali.

Diwali sweets in Gujarat

In Gujarat, Diwali runs straight into Bestu Varas, the New Year, when families visit each other with boxes of homemade sweets and farsan. Many of these sweets set in a thali and are cut into squares, which makes them easy to pack and share.

The 12 sweets

What each one is, what it is made of, and how long it keeps.

  1. 01 of 12

    Mohanthal

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps 2+ weeks

    Coarse gram flour roasted in ghee, cooked with syrup and khoya, set in a tray and cut into squares. Rich, grainy and fragrant with saffron.

    Made with: Coarse gram flour, ghee, milk, sugar syrup, khoya, saffron, cardamom

  2. 02 of 12

    Magaj

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps 2+ weeks

    A gram-flour fudge made without syrup: roasted besan and ghee are mixed with powdered sugar and set. Simpler than mohanthal.

    Made with: Gram flour, ghee, powdered sugar, nuts

  3. 03 of 12

    Sukhdi / Golpapdi

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps 2+ weeks

    Wheat flour roasted in ghee and stirred with jaggery off the heat, then set and cut. Ready in about 15 minutes.

    Made with: Wheat flour, ghee, jaggery

  4. 04 of 12

    Ghughra

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadKeeps 2+ weeks

    Gujarat's karanji: a crimped pastry filled with roasted semolina or khoya, coconut, sugar and nuts, then fried.

    Made with: Maida, semolina or khoya, coconut, sugar, nuts

  5. 05 of 12

    Adadiya Pak

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps 2+ weeks

    A dense, spiced fudge of urad dal flour, ghee and edible gum, eaten through winter for warmth and strength.

    Made with: Urad dal flour, ghee, edible gum, dry ginger, nutmeg, sugar

  6. 06 of 12

    Kopra Pak

    Barfi & fudgeKeeps about a week

    Coconut barfi cooked with sugar and milk, soft and fragrant with cardamom.

    Made with: Coconut, sugar, milk, cardamom

  7. 07 of 12

    Suterfeni

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    Fine, thread-like nests of layered pastry, fried and dusted with sugar. Eaten as is or soaked in warm sweetened milk.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, rice flour, powdered sugar

  8. 08 of 12

    Basundi

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Slow-reduced sweet milk with nuts and cardamom, served chilled with puri or on its own.

    Made with: Full-fat milk, sugar, cardamom, nuts

  9. 09 of 12

    Shrikhand

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Chilled, silky strained yogurt with saffron and cardamom; a Gujarati thali favorite with puri.

    Made with: Hung curd, sugar, saffron, cardamom, pistachios

  10. 10 of 12

    Lapsi

    Halwa & puddingBest eaten fresh

    Broken wheat roasted in ghee and cooked with jaggery or sugar. Considered auspicious, it is made for new beginnings such as Bestu Varas.

    Made with: Broken wheat, ghee, jaggery or sugar, cardamom

  11. 11 of 12

    Dudhi Halwo

    Halwa & puddingRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Grated bottle gourd cooked down in milk and sugar until glossy and pale green.

    Made with: Bottle gourd, milk, sugar, ghee, cardamom

  12. 12 of 12

    Kansar

    Halwa & puddingBest eaten fresh

    A traditional sweet of coarse wheat cooked with jaggery, served hot with ghee and sugar poured on top at the table.

    Made with: Coarse wheat flour, jaggery or sugar, ghee

Related sweets in other states

  • Ghughra: a sealed half-moon pastry with a dry sweet filling, fried crisp. Related: Karanji (Maharashtra), Kajjikayalu (Andhra Pradesh), Karjikai (Karnataka), Somas (Tamil Nadu), Pedakiya (Bihar).
  • Suterfeni: hair-fine fried pastry nests, eaten dry or with milk. Related: Feeni (Rajasthan).
  • Mohanthal / Magaj: besan roasted in ghee and set with sugar. Related: Mysore Pak (Karnataka), Mysore Pak (Tamil Nadu), Mohanthal (Rajasthan), Besan Chakki (Rajasthan), Besan Burfi (Burfi).
  • Basundi: milk cooked down for hours into kova, peda or kunda. Related: Palakova (Andhra Pradesh), Palkova (Tamil Nadu), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Maharashtra).

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular Gujarati Diwali sweets?

Gujarati Diwali sweets are rich in ghee and gram flour: mohanthal, magaj and sukhdi are made at home in big batches, alongside ghughra, kopra pak and the winter-warming adadiya pak. Suterfeni, basundi and shrikhand round out the festive table. Lapsi and kansar are cooked for auspicious starts, including Bestu Varas, the Gujarati New Year the day after Diwali.

Which Gujarati Diwali sweets keep longest for gifting?

Mohanthal, Magaj, Sukhdi / Golpapdi, Ghughra, Adadiya Pak and Suterfeni keep for two weeks or more in an airtight container, so they can be made first and are the safest to gift or courier.

Which Gujarati Diwali sweets need to be made last or refrigerated?

Basundi, Shrikhand, Lapsi, Dudhi Halwo and Kansar are milk-, chhena-, curd- or fresh-coconut based or best served warm. Make them a day or two ahead at most, refrigerate, and serve within a few days.

Which other states make similar sweets?

Crimped crescent pastries: Karanji (Maharashtra), Kajjikayalu (Andhra Pradesh), Karjikai (Karnataka), Somas (Tamil Nadu), Pedakiya (Bihar). Fine pastry threads: Feeni (Rajasthan). Gram flour and ghee fudges: Mysore Pak (Karnataka), Mysore Pak (Tamil Nadu), Mohanthal (Rajasthan), Besan Chakki (Rajasthan), Besan Burfi (Burfi). Slow-reduced milk sweets: Palakova (Andhra Pradesh), Palkova (Tamil Nadu), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Maharashtra).
