12 Traditional Maharashtrian Diwali Sweets

Maharashtrian Diwali centers on faral, a tray of homemade sweets and snacks prepared in the days before Lakshmi Puja.

By Prashant Dhingra

See all 12 sweets Keeping guide

In short

Maharashtrian Diwali centers on faral, a tray of homemade sweets and snacks prepared in the days before Lakshmi Puja. The sweet half usually holds anarsa, karanji, shankarpali, chirote and besan, rava or naral ladoos. Festive meals add puran poli, basundi, shrikhand and sheera, especially on Padwa and Bhau Beej.

Diwali sweets in Maharashtra

Families start making faral a week or more ahead, and plates of it are exchanged with neighbors and offered to every visitor. Anarsa is the most demanding item: the rice must be soaked for days and the dough rested, so many households begin with it. Basundi and shrikhand are typically served at the Padwa and Bhau Beej meals.

The 12 sweets

What each one is, what it is made of, and how long it keeps.

  1. 01 of 12

    Anarsa

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    A crisp-edged, chewy fried disc made from rice flour and jaggery dough rested for several days, then pressed onto poppy seeds. It is the signature faral sweet and the hardest to get right.

    Made with: Soaked rice flour, jaggery, poppy seeds, ghee

  2. 02 of 12

    Karanji

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadKeeps 2+ weeks

    A crescent-shaped fried pastry filled with roasted dry coconut, sugar and cardamom. Sealed edges are crimped by hand or with a cutter so the filling stays dry and crisp.

    Made with: Maida or fine rava, dry coconut, sugar, poppy seeds, cardamom

  3. 03 of 12

    Shankarpali

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    Small diamond-cut sweet crackers, fried until golden and crunchy. They are easy to make in large batches, which is why every faral box has them.

    Made with: Maida, milk, sugar, ghee

  4. 04 of 12

    Chirote

    Fried & crispKeeps 2+ weeks

    A layered, flaky pastry rolled with a ghee and rice-flour paste between the layers, fried and dusted with powdered sugar or dipped in light syrup.

    Made with: Maida, ghee, rice flour paste (satva), powdered sugar

  5. 05 of 12

    Besan Ladoo

    LadooKeeps 2+ weeks

    Gram flour roasted slowly in ghee until it smells nutty, then mixed with powdered sugar and shaped. Patient roasting is what removes the raw taste.

    Made with: Gram flour, ghee, powdered sugar, cardamom

  6. 06 of 12

    Rava Ladoo

    LadooKeeps about a week

    Roasted semolina and coconut bound with sugar syrup or ghee. Softer than besan ladoo, and quick enough to make on the day.

    Made with: Semolina, coconut, sugar, ghee, cashews, raisins

  7. 07 of 12

    Naral Ladoo

    LadooRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Coconut ladoo, cooked with sugar or jaggery and often milk or khoya. With fresh coconut they are moist and should be refrigerated.

    Made with: Fresh or dry coconut, sugar or jaggery, milk or khoya

  8. 08 of 12

    Puran Poli

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    A soft flatbread stuffed with sweet chana dal and jaggery (puran), cooked on a tava and served with ghee or katachi amti.

    Made with: Chana dal, jaggery, wheat flour, cardamom, nutmeg, ghee

  9. 09 of 12

    Basundi

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Milk simmered for a long time until thick and creamy, sweetened and served chilled with nuts. A favorite for Padwa and Bhau Beej meals.

    Made with: Full-fat milk, sugar, cardamom, nutmeg, nuts

  10. 10 of 12

    Shrikhand

    Milk & chhenaRefrigerate, 2 to 4 days

    Strained yogurt whisked with sugar, saffron and cardamom until silky. Served chilled with puris; the mango version is amrakhand.

    Made with: Hung curd, sugar, saffron, cardamom

  11. 11 of 12

    Modak

    Stuffed pastry & flatbreadBest eaten fresh

    Steamed rice-flour dumplings with a coconut and jaggery filling, best known from Ganesh Chaturthi. Fried modaks keep longer than steamed ones.

    Made with: Rice flour, fresh coconut, jaggery, cardamom

  12. 12 of 12

    Sheera

    Halwa & puddingBest eaten fresh

    A quick semolina halwa cooked in ghee and milk, often made as naivedya (offering) for puja and served warm.

    Made with: Semolina, ghee, sugar, milk, saffron, cardamom

Related sweets in other states

  • Anarsa: soaked rice flour and jaggery dough, rested, then fried. Related: Ariselu (Andhra Pradesh), Adhirasam (Tamil Nadu), Anarsa (Bihar).
  • Karanji: a sealed half-moon pastry with a dry sweet filling, fried crisp. Related: Kajjikayalu (Andhra Pradesh), Karjikai (Karnataka), Ghughra (Gujarat), Somas (Tamil Nadu), Pedakiya (Bihar).
  • Puran Poli: chana dal and jaggery filling inside a thin flatbread. Related: Bobbatlu (Andhra Pradesh), Obbattu / Holige (Karnataka).
  • Chirote: many thin layers fried crisp, then sugared or soaked. Related: Chiroti (Karnataka), Khaja (Bihar), Kakinada Kaja (Andhra Pradesh), Madatha Kaja (Andhra Pradesh).
  • Basundi: milk cooked down for hours into kova, peda or kunda. Related: Palakova (Andhra Pradesh), Palkova (Tamil Nadu), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Gujarat).

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular Maharashtrian Diwali sweets?

Maharashtrian Diwali centers on faral, a tray of homemade sweets and snacks prepared in the days before Lakshmi Puja. The sweet half usually holds anarsa, karanji, shankarpali, chirote and besan, rava or naral ladoos. Festive meals add puran poli, basundi, shrikhand and sheera, especially on Padwa and Bhau Beej.

Which Maharashtrian Diwali sweets keep longest for gifting?

Anarsa, Karanji, Shankarpali, Chirote and Besan Ladoo keep for two weeks or more in an airtight container, so they can be made first and are the safest to gift or courier.

Which Maharashtrian Diwali sweets need to be made last or refrigerated?

Naral Ladoo, Puran Poli, Basundi, Shrikhand, Modak and Sheera are milk-, chhena-, curd- or fresh-coconut based or best served warm. Make them a day or two ahead at most, refrigerate, and serve within a few days.

Which other states make similar sweets?

Rice and jaggery fried discs: Ariselu (Andhra Pradesh), Adhirasam (Tamil Nadu), Anarsa (Bihar). Crimped crescent pastries: Kajjikayalu (Andhra Pradesh), Karjikai (Karnataka), Ghughra (Gujarat), Somas (Tamil Nadu), Pedakiya (Bihar). Sweet-stuffed flatbreads: Bobbatlu (Andhra Pradesh), Obbattu / Holige (Karnataka). Layered fried pastries: Chiroti (Karnataka), Khaja (Bihar), Kakinada Kaja (Andhra Pradesh), Madatha Kaja (Andhra Pradesh). Slow-reduced milk sweets: Palakova (Andhra Pradesh), Palkova (Tamil Nadu), Dharwad Peda (Karnataka), Belagavi Kunda (Karnataka), Basundi (Gujarat).
