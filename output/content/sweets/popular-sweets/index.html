Diwali Sweets of India Eight states, 120 sweets, one festival.

From Maharashtrian faral and Tamil bakshanam to Bengali chhena sweets and Bihari thekua: what each state makes for Diwali, what it is made of, and how long it keeps.

By Prashant Dhingra

Browse by state Find a sweet

In short

Diwali sweets change from state to state: faral in Maharashtra, bakshanam in Tamil Nadu, chhena sweets in Bengal, ghee-rich mohanthal and ghevar in Gujarat and Rajasthan, and thekua and khaja in Bihar. Many are regional versions of the same idea, such as crimped crescent pastries, jaggery rice discs and gram-flour fudges. This guide covers 96 sweets from eight states plus twelve laddoos and twelve burfis, 120 in all, with what each is made of and how long it keeps.

Diwali sweets by state

Twelve traditional sweets from each of eight states.

  • Maharashtra · 12 sweets 12 Traditional Maharashtrian Diwali Sweets Maharashtrian Diwali centers on faral, a tray of homemade sweets and snacks prepared in the days before Lakshmi Puja.
  • Andhra Pradesh · 12 sweets 12 Traditional Andhra Diwali Sweets Andhra's Deepavali sweets mix jaggery-rich home classics with famous town specialities.
  • West Bengal · 12 sweets 12 Types of Bengali Diwali Sweets Bengali sweets are built on chhena, fresh curdled-milk cheese, kneaded and either simmered in syrup (rasgulla, rajbhog, cham cham), fried (pantua, langcha) or gently cooked (sandesh).
  • Gujarat · 12 sweets 12 Traditional Gujarati Diwali Sweets Gujarati Diwali sweets are rich in ghee and gram flour: mohanthal, magaj and sukhdi are made at home in big batches, alongside ghughra, kopra pak and the winter-warming adadiya pak.
  • Karnataka · 12 sweets 12 Traditional Karnataka Diwali Sweets Karnataka's Deepavali sweets range from palace classics to town specialities: Mysore pak, Dharwad peda and Belagavi kunda sit beside home favorites such as obbattu (holige), chiroti, karjikai and kobbari burfi.
  • Rajasthan · 12 sweets 12 Traditional Rajasthani Diwali Sweets Rajasthani Diwali sweets are rich with ghee, mawa and gram flour: ghevar and rabri ghevar, Jodhpur's mawa kachori, mohanthal, besan chakki, churma ladoo and mishri mawa.
  • Bihar · 12 sweets 12 Traditional Bihari Diwali Sweets Bihari festival sweets are rustic and long-keeping: thekua, khaja, anarsa, tilkut, lai and khurma sit beside syrup sweets such as balushahi, malpua and kala jamun.
  • Tamil Nadu · 12 sweets 12 Traditional Tamil Nadu Diwali Sweets Tamil Deepavali begins before dawn with an oil bath, new clothes and a plate of homemade sweets (bakshanam).

Diwali sweets by type

The two sweets every Indian region shares.

  • By type · 12 sweets 12 Types of Diwali Laddoos Laddoos are the easiest Diwali sweet to make and share: besan, motichoor and boondi ladoos are the classics, rava and coconut the quick ones.
  • By type · 12 sweets 12 Types of Diwali Burfi Burfi is a fudge-like sweet set in a tray and cut into squares or diamonds, which makes it the backbone of Diwali gift boxes.

Making Diwali sweets at home

  • Plan by keeping time. Start about a week ahead with fried and ghee sweets (anarsa, karanji, chirote, thekua), then laddoos and burfis, and leave milk sweets for the last day.
  • Learn the syrup tests. Press a drop of cooled syrup between finger and thumb: a sticky feel suits soaking sweets such as gulab jamun and jalebi, a single thread suits boondi and coconut sweets, and two threads or a soft ball suits burfis that must set.
  • Roast flours slowly. Besan, atta and rava need low heat and constant stirring in ghee until they smell nutty; rushing leaves a raw taste.
  • Make khoya ahead. Reducing milk for mawa takes an hour or more, so do it the day before and keep it chilled.
  • Fry at the right heat. Pastries such as karanji and chirote fry at medium-low so the layers cook through without browning too fast.

A 7-day Diwali sweet plan

  1. Day 1: fix the menu, shop, and soak rice for anarsa, ariselu or adhirasam.
  2. Day 2: fried, long-keeping sweets such as shankarpali, chirote, thekua and khurma.
  3. Day 3: stuffed pastries: karanji, ghughra, somas or pedakiya.
  4. Day 4: laddoos: besan, rava, boondi or dry fruit.
  5. Day 5: burfis and fudges such as kaju katli, Mysore pak and mohanthal; make khoya for the milk sweets.
  6. Day 6: syrup sweets (gulab jamun, jangiri, badusha), then pack gift boxes.
  7. Diwali day: milk sweets made fresh, such as basundi, shrikhand or sandesh, and the puja naivedya.

Gifting and storing sweets

  • For gift boxes and couriers, choose sweets marked "Keeps 2+ weeks": kaju katli, badam and anjeer burfi, Mysore pak, mohanthal, besan ladoo, anarsa, chirote and pootharekulu travel well.
  • Keep syrup and dry sweets apart so crisp sweets stay crisp, and line boxes with butter paper.
  • Chill milk sweets such as sandesh, rasgulla, basundi and shrikhand, and tell recipients to refrigerate them.
  • Label homemade boxes with the date and any nuts, so guests with allergies can choose safely.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular Diwali sweets in India?

Kaju katli, besan and motichoor laddoos, gulab jamun, rasgulla, Mysore pak and regional favorites such as anarsa, adhirasam, mohanthal, thekua and sandesh. Each state has its own core list.

Which Diwali sweets keep longest for gifting?

Ghee-, gram-flour-, nut- and jaggery-based sweets keep best: 46 of the 120 sweets here keep for two weeks or more, including anarsa, karanji, chirote, besan ladoo, kaju katli, Mysore pak, mohanthal, thekua, tilkut and pootharekulu.

Which Diwali sweets need refrigeration?

Anything made with fresh milk, chhena, curd, khoya or fresh coconut: rasgulla, sandesh, rasmalai, basundi, shrikhand, palkova and most mawa burfis. Keep them chilled and eat within a few days.

Why do different states have such similar sweets?

Many sweets share a technique and differ in name and detail. The crimped crescent pastry is karanji in Maharashtra, ghughra in Gujarat, karjikai in Karnataka, kajjikayalu in Andhra, somas in Tamil Nadu and pedakiya in Bihar.

When should I start making Diwali sweets?

About a week ahead: begin with the long-keeping fried and ghee sweets, move to laddoos and burfis two or three days before, and make milk and chhena sweets the day before or on Diwali itself.
