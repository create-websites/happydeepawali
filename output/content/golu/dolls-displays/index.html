What dolls go in a Golu?

Golu (also called Kolu, Bommai Golu or Bombe Habba) is a Navaratri display of dolls arranged on an odd number of steps. Deities usually take the top steps, saints and sages sit below them, and royal, wedding, village and everyday scenes fill the lower steps. Most collections start with a Marapachi doll pair and a few deity sets, then grow a little every year.

Golu dolls: quick answers

What dolls do you need for a traditional Golu?

Most families start with a Marapachi doll pair, a few deity figures for the upper steps (for example Dashavatara, Devi, Lakshmi or Saraswati sets) and one or two everyday scenes such as a Chettiar shop or a wedding set. Collections then grow a little each year.

How are Golu dolls arranged on the steps?

Golu is usually set on an odd number of steps. A common pattern places deities on the top steps, saints and sages below them, and royal, wedding, village and everyday-life scenes on the lower steps. Arrangements vary by family, region and sampradaya.

What are Marapachi dolls?

Marapachi dolls are a carved wooden doll pair, traditionally given to a bride and kept for years. Families dress them in new clothes and jewellery for Navaratri and give them a prominent place in the Golu.

Are handmade clay dolls better than modern Golu dolls?

Handmade clay and papier-mâché dolls carry traditional craft and feel, but are more fragile. Modern figures are often sturdier and easier to store. Many families mix both, choosing heirloom pieces for meaning and modern sets for durability.

How should heirloom Golu dolls be stored after Navaratri?

Dust each doll gently, wrap it individually in soft cloth or acid-free paper, and store it in a dry, ventilated box away from damp, heat and pests. Check older dolls before the next season and repair chips or loose parts early.
