What are the daily rituals of Golu?

Each day of Navaratri, families light a lamp before the Golu, offer flowers and naivedyam such as sundal, recite prayers or sing, and perform aarti, usually in the evening when guests visit. Saraswati Puja and Ayudha Puja are kept near the end of the festival, and Vijayadashami brings it to a close.

Golu rituals: quick answers

How do you begin Golu on the first day of Navaratri?

Most families set up the steps and dolls on or just before the first day of Navaratri. Many install a kalash on the top step, then light a lamp, offer flowers and naivedyam such as sundal, and say a short prayer. Family custom decides the details.

What is the daily Golu puja?

A typical daily puja includes lighting a lamp before the Golu, offering flowers and naivedyam, reciting prayers or singing, and performing aarti, usually in the evening when guests visit. It can be elaborate or very short on busy days.

What should you do if you miss a day of Golu puja?

Simply resume the next day. Missing a day because of illness, travel or work is common, and most families continue the observance rather than starting over. If your family follows a specific rule, ask an elder or your priest.

What happens on Saraswati Puja and Ayudha Puja?

Near the end of Navaratri, books and musical instruments are placed before Saraswati and worshipped, and on Ayudha Puja tools, vehicles and the instruments of daily work are honoured. Learning or work often resumes on Vijayadashami.

Why is one doll put to sleep after Golu?

On Vijayadashami many families lay one doll, often from the Marapachi pair, down to rest as a symbolic close to the festival, with a prayer that the Golu will return the next year. The display is then taken down at a convenient time.
