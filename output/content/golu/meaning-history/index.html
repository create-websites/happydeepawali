What is Golu?

Golu (also Kolu or Bommai Golu, and Bombe Habba in Karnataka) is a South Indian Navaratri tradition in which families arrange dolls and figurines on an odd number of steps, worship the Goddess each day, and invite friends and relatives to visit. The display usually goes up at the start of Navaratri and is taken down after Vijayadashami.

Golu meaning and history: quick answers

What does the word Golu mean?

Golu, or Kolu, is usually explained as a royal court or assembly, and Bommai means doll, so Bommai Golu is often described as a court of dolls. In Karnataka the festival is called Bombe Habba, the festival of dolls.

When does Golu start and end?

Most families set up the Golu on or just before the first day of Navaratri and keep it until Vijayadashami, the tenth day. The display is usually taken down after a final puja, and dates follow the lunar calendar, so they change each year.

Which goddesses are worshipped during Golu?

Golu honours the Goddess through Navaratri. Many families dedicate the nine nights to Durga, Lakshmi and Saraswati, often three nights each, with Saraswati Puja near the end of the festival.

Is Golu related to Diwali?

No. Golu is part of Navaratri, which ends with Vijayadashami. Diwali is a separate festival that usually falls a few weeks later, although both belong to the same autumn festival season.

Where is Golu celebrated?

Golu is kept in Tamil Nadu, in Karnataka as Bombe Habba, and in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana as Bommala Koluvu, as well as by South Indian families around the world. Customs vary by region, family and sampradaya.
