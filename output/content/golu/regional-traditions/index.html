Golu Regional Traditions One festival, many homes.

20 guides to how the Navaratri doll festival changes across South India and the world: Bommai Golu in Tamil Nadu, Bombe Habba in Karnataka, Bommala Koluvu in the Telugu states, and Golu in the diaspora.

Is Golu the same across South India?

The heart of the tradition is shared: dolls arranged on steps during Navaratri, worship of the Goddess and visits between homes. The names and details change by region: Golu or Bommai Golu in Tamil Nadu, Bombe Habba in Karnataka, where the pattada gombe doll pair takes pride of place, and Bommala Koluvu in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Within each region, family and community customs vary further.

Regional Golu traditions: quick answers

What is Bombe Habba?

Bombe Habba, the festival of dolls, is Karnataka’s name for the Navaratri doll display. Many families give pride of place to the pattada gombe, a pair of wooden dolls often received at marriage, and the festival is closely associated with Mysuru Dasara.

What is Bommala Koluvu?

Bommala Koluvu, roughly an assembly or court of dolls, is the Telugu name used in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana for a doll display similar to Golu. Timing and customs vary by region and family.

How is Golu different in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka?

The core is shared: dolls arranged on steps, worship of the Goddess and visits between homes. The names differ (Bommai Golu and Bombe Habba), as do the central doll pair (Marapachi dolls in Tamil homes, pattada gombe in Karnataka), the songs and some of the food.

Do Iyer and Iyengar families celebrate Golu differently?

Both share the same basic Golu, but the prayers, songs and deities a family emphasises can differ, reflecting the Smarta tradition of many Iyer families and the Sri Vaishnava tradition of Iyengar families. Individual family custom matters as much as community.

How is Golu celebrated outside India?

Families in the United States, the United Kingdom, Singapore, Malaysia and elsewhere keep Golu at home, adapt the steps to smaller spaces, visit each other at weekends, and join community and temple Golus. Many also share their displays online with relatives in India.
