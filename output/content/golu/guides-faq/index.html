Is there one correct way to keep Golu?

No. Most households share a common core: dolls arranged on an odd number of steps, a daily lamp and offering during Navaratri, sundal for visitors, and a respectful close on Vijayadashami. Beyond that, the number of steps, which dolls are used and how long the display stays up are shaped by family, region and sampradaya.

Golu questions: quick answers

Why are Golu steps odd in number?

Golu is usually set on three, five, seven or nine steps. Odd numbers are traditionally considered auspicious, and nine steps are often linked to the nine nights of Navaratri. Many families simply use the number of steps that fits their space.

Can you keep Golu for fewer than nine days?

Yes. Many families shorten the display because of travel, work or space, and keep the core of the observance: setting up with care, a daily lamp and offering, welcoming guests and closing respectfully. Family custom guides the details.

Can beginners start a Golu without inherited dolls?

Yes. A new Golu can begin with a few dolls, such as a Marapachi pair and one or two deity figures, on a small number of steps or even a shelf. The collection and the family customs can grow from year to year.

Should you add new dolls to the Golu every year?

Many families like to add at least one new doll each year, but it is a custom rather than a requirement. What matters most is caring for the dolls you have and the stories they hold.

What should you do with broken Golu dolls?

Repair a doll if you can. If it cannot be repaired, families retire it respectfully according to their own custom, for example by storing it separately or returning clay dolls to water or earth.
