How do you set up a Golu?

Choose a spot with room for guests, build an odd number of sturdy steps (usually three, five, seven or nine) and cover them with cloth. Place the kalash and main deities on the top step, then arrange saints, story scenes and everyday life on the lower steps. Most families finish the setup on or just before the first day of Navaratri.

Golu setup: quick answers

How many steps should a Golu have?

Golu is traditionally set on an odd number of steps, most often three, five, seven or nine. Choose the number that suits your space and your collection; a small, well-arranged Golu is just as meaningful as a large one.

How are Golu dolls arranged on the steps?

A common order places the kalash and main deities on the top step, other deities and saints below them, then people, festivals and story scenes, with animals, village life and everyday scenes on the lowest steps. Families adapt this order to their own dolls and customs.

Where should the kalash be placed in a Golu?

Many families place the kalash on the top step, at the centre or beside the main deity, so that it is the focus of the daily puja. Make sure it sits securely and is not knocked by lamps or decorations.

Which direction should a Golu face?

Many families prefer the Golu to face east or north. When the room does not allow this, most simply choose a clean, respectful spot where the display is stable and guests can see it easily.

When should you set up the Golu?

Many families set up the Golu on Mahalaya Amavasya, the day before Navaratri begins, or on the morning of the first day. Allow time to build and test the steps before placing the dolls.
