Golu at a glance

What is Golu?

Golu (also Kolu or Bommai Golu, Bombe Habba in Karnataka and Bommala Koluvu in the Telugu states) is the South Indian Navaratri tradition of arranging dolls on an odd number of steps, worshipping the Goddess each evening, and inviting friends and family to visit.

Guests admire the display, often sing a song, and leave with sundal and thamboolam. On Vijayadashami many families lay one doll down to rest before the Golu is packed away until next year.

  • 3 to 9steps, usually odd
  • 1 kalashoften on the top step

The steps of a Golu

A common order moves from the divine at the top to everyday life at the bottom. Tap a step to read its guide.

  1. Step 1Kalash and the principal deitiesThe top step usually holds the kalash and the main forms of the Goddess, the focus of the daily puja.
  2. Step 2Gods and their avatarsVishnu’s Dashavatara, Shiva and Parvati, Lakshmi and Saraswati sets often fill the next steps.
  3. Step 3Saints, sages and acharyasSaints, poets and teachers are placed with respect below the deities.
  4. Step 4Scenes from the epicsRamayana and Krishna Leela scenes turn a step into a story told in dolls.
  5. Step 5Royal courts and celebrationsRaja-Rani durbars, wedding processions and festivals bring human life onto the steps.
  6. Step 6Village life, markets and animalsFarmers, potters, shops and animals usually fill the lowest steps.

Families adapt this order to their own dolls and customs. See how to arrange Golu dolls by level.

How do you start a Golu?

Choose a spot with room for guests, build three, five, seven or nine sturdy steps and cover them with cloth. Place the kalash and main deities at the top, arrange the rest of your dolls step by step, light a lamp and offer sundal on the first evening of Navaratri. Read the step-by-step setup guide.

Golu: quick answers

What is Golu?

Golu (also Kolu or Bommai Golu; Bombe Habba in Karnataka and Bommala Koluvu in the Telugu states) is a South Indian Navaratri tradition of arranging dolls on an odd number of steps, worshipping the Goddess each day, and inviting friends and family to visit.

How are the steps of a Golu arranged?

A common order places the kalash and main deities on the top step, other deities and saints below them, then scenes from the epics and human life, with village life and animals on the lowest steps. Families adapt this to their own dolls and customs.

When is Golu kept?

Golu is set up on or just before the first day of Navaratri and kept until Vijayadashami, the tenth day, when many families lay one doll down to rest before taking the display down.

What is served to guests at a Golu?

Visitors are usually given sundal, a dish of tempered legumes offered first to the Goddess, along with thamboolam such as betel leaves, fruit, turmeric and kumkum.
