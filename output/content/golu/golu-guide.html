What is Golu?

Golu (also Kolu or Bommai Golu, Bombe Habba in Karnataka and Bommala Koluvu in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana) is a South Indian Navaratri tradition. Families arrange dolls on an odd number of steps, worship the Goddess each evening, and welcome friends and relatives, who leave with sundal and thamboolam. The display goes up at the start of Navaratri and comes down after Vijayadashami.

Golu: quick answers

What is Golu?

Golu (also Kolu or Bommai Golu; Bombe Habba in Karnataka and Bommala Koluvu in the Telugu states) is a South Indian Navaratri tradition of arranging dolls on an odd number of steps, worshipping the Goddess each day, and inviting friends and family to visit.

Where should a beginner start?

Start with the meaning of Golu, then the setup guides for a simple three or five-step display. A Marapachi pair and a few deity dolls are enough for a first year; the collection can grow over time.

How long is Golu kept?

Most families set up the Golu on or just before the first day of Navaratri and keep it until Vijayadashami, the tenth day, before taking it down after a final puja.

What do guests receive at a Golu?

Visitors usually receive sundal, first offered to the Goddess, along with thamboolam such as betel leaves, fruit, turmeric and kumkum, and often a small return gift.
