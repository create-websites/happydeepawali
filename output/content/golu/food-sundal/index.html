What is sundal?

Sundal is a South Indian dish of boiled legumes, such as chickpeas, green gram, cowpeas or peanuts, tempered with mustard seeds, urad dal, curry leaves and chillies and often finished with grated coconut. During Navaratri it is offered to the Goddess as naivedyam and then shared with everyone who visits the Golu.

Sundal and Golu food: quick answers

Why is sundal served during Golu?

Sundal is simple, nourishing and easy to make in quantity, so it suits a festival where visitors come every evening. It is first offered to the Goddess as naivedyam and then shared with guests as prasadam.

Which legumes are used to make sundal?

Common choices include white and black chickpeas, whole green gram, cowpeas, peanuts and dried white peas. Many families make a different sundal on each night of Navaratri, and sweet corn or mixed legumes are popular modern options.

Is sundal made without onion and garlic?

Yes. A traditional sundal is tempered with mustard seeds, urad dal, curry leaves, chillies and asafoetida and finished with coconut, with no onion or garlic, which keeps it suitable as a naivedyam offering.

Can sundal be made ahead?

The legumes can be soaked and cooked in advance and refrigerated. Temper and add fresh coconut close to serving time, keep cooked sundal chilled, and use it within a day.

How is sundal given to guests at a Golu?

Guests usually receive sundal after viewing the Golu, often in a small cup or packet to take home, and many households offer it together with thamboolam. Customs vary by family and region.
