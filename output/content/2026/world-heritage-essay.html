The first Deepawali of a world heritage

On 8 November 2026 India lights its lamps for a festival the rest of the world has just officially recognised. Why that matters, and why it changes nothing at all.

By Prashant Dhingra · 2 October 2026

The dates, first: Deepawali (Diwali) 2026 falls on Sunday 8 November 2026. Lakshmi Puja muhurat in New Delhi is 5:54 PM to 7:50 PM IST. The five days run from Dhanteras on 6 November to Bhai Dooj on 11 November, with Govardhan Puja on 10 November, and Dev Deepawali in Varanasi follows on 24 November. It is the first Diwali since UNESCO inscribed Deepavali as intangible cultural heritage in December 2025.

Last December, in a hall inside the Red Fort, a UNESCO committee added Deepavali to its list of the intangible cultural heritage of humanity. It was India's sixteenth entry and, fittingly for a festival that has always been about the household, it was filed under social practices, rituals and festive events. Nothing about Diwali itself changed that day. What changed is that the thing millions of families do every autumn, without thinking of it as heritage, was named as something worth keeping.

That is a good frame for Deepawali 2026. Not as a spectacle to be measured, though it is one, but as a set of small acts repeated in millions of homes: sweeping out the year, buying one new thing, drawing a pattern at the door, putting a lamp where the dark is.

Why the lamps

The story most of north India tells is the homecoming. Twenty days after Dussehra, when Ram has defeated Ravana, he returns to Ayodhya with Sita and Lakshman at the end of fourteen years of exile, on the darkest night of the month. The people of the city light rows of lamps so they can find their way home. Every diya on a windowsill is a small re-enactment of that welcome.

But Diwali has never had only one story, which is part of why it travels so well. In the same five days Krishna defeats Narakasura and lifts Govardhan hill; Lakshmi, who rose from the churning of the ocean, is welcomed into every home that is clean and lit; Bengal worships Kali on the new-moon night; Sikhs remember Guru Hargobind's release on Bandi Chhor Divas; Jains mark the nirvana of Mahavira. The lamp is the one thing they share.

The numbers, and their limits

At the ninth Deepotsav on 19 October 2025, Ayodhya lit 26,17,215 diyas across 56 ghats of the Saryu, a count verified by Guinness World Records, while 2,128 Vedacharyas performed a maha aarti together and about 1,100 drones drew scenes from the Ramayana in the sky. Each year the count rises, and each year it makes a fine photograph.

But the more interesting number is the one nobody counts: the single lamp facing south for Yama on Dhanteras, the five lamps on a village wall, the electric string some flat in a tower block hangs on its balcony. Heritage, in UNESCO's sense, is precisely this kind of practice : transmitted inside families, varying from street to street, alive because people keep doing it.

How 2026 falls

This year the calendar is unusually tidy for the main night and slightly untidy around it. Lakshmi Puja is on Sunday 8 November in India and abroad. But Amavasya runs into the morning of the 9th, so in most Indian cities Govardhan Puja moves to Tuesday the 10th and Bhai Dooj to Wednesday the 11th, leaving a quiet Monday in the middle.

Fifteen days later, on Kartik Purnima, Varanasi has the last word. Dev Deepawali on Tuesday 24 November lines every ghat of the Ganga with lamps, the gods' own Diwali, and it may be the most beautiful night of the whole season.

Keeping it

If the recognition means anything for an ordinary household, it is permission to do the old things properly. Cleaning before Diwali clears out the old year: it is a way of removing negativity and making room for Lakshmi. Do the deep work in the last week of October so the days just before 6 November are free for shopping and decorating. Choosing local potters, florists, sweet-makers and craftspeople keeps the money in your own neighbourhood. Set aside a share of the festival budget for people who need it: sweets, clothes or diyas for families who cannot buy them. Think about the person, not the price: a puja set for parents, a small personalised piece of jewellery for a sibling, a warm blanket for grandparents, or something handcrafted and a box of local delicacies. The thought behind it is what is remembered.

And on the night, open the door. That is the oldest instruction of all: Lakshmi visits the house that is lit and waiting.

Deepawali 2026: questions people ask

When is Diwali in 2026?

The main day of Diwali, the night of Lakshmi Puja, is Sunday 8 November 2026, on Kartik Amavasya. The festival opens with Dhanteras on Friday 6 November and closes with Bhai Dooj on Wednesday 11 November.

What is the Lakshmi Puja muhurat for Diwali 2026?

Drik Panchang gives 5:54 PM to 7:50 PM IST for New Delhi on 8 November, the Vrishabha Kaal inside Pradosh Kaal (5:31 PM to 8:09 PM). Jaipur is 6:03 to 7:59 PM, Chennai 6:07 to 8:09 PM and Pune 6:24 to 8:24 PM. Check a panchang for your own city.

Is Diwali 2026 on 8 or 9 November?

8 November. Amavasya also touches the sunrise of 9 November, but Lakshmi Puja is done in the evening, and the Amavasya that covers the evening puja window falls on the 8th.

When is Govardhan Puja in 2026?

Drik Panchang lists Tuesday 10 November for most Indian cities, because Pratipada only reaches sunrise on the 10th. Some calendars, some cities and most of the western hemisphere keep it on Monday 9 November.

When is Bhai Dooj in 2026?

Wednesday 11 November 2026, on Kartik Shukla Dwitiya.

When is Dev Deepawali 2026 in Varanasi?

Tuesday 24 November 2026, on Kartik Purnima, fifteen days after Diwali. A few listings give 25 November, so confirm locally before booking a boat.

Is Diwali a UNESCO heritage?

Yes. Deepavali was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity on 10 December 2025, making Diwali 2026 the first since that recognition.

Is Diwali on the same day in the USA and India in 2026?

Yes, the Lakshmi Puja night is Sunday 8 November in both, though the muhurat follows local sunset. The days around it can differ, so use a panchang set to your city.
