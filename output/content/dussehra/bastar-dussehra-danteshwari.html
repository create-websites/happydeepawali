Bastar Dussehra: The 75-Day Festival of Goddess Danteshwari

Discover the 75-day Bastar Dussehra festival, focusing on Goddess Danteshwari, tribal customs, and local rituals.

Why this guide matters: Bastar Dussehra breaks the stereotype of Rama, Ravana, and effigy burning being the focal point of every Dussehra celebration, instead putting the spotlight on Goddess Danteshwari and the rich ritual traditions of Bastar

India’s unusually long Dussehra cycle

Bastar Dussehra runs for about 75 days, making it one of the longest celebrations linked to Dussehra. Instead of cramming all rituals into one final evening, they unfold gradually in stages.

The prolonged timeline represents a sacred calendar specific to the region, encompassing unique customs and communal duties.

Goddess Danteshwari at the center

In contrast to the Rama-Ravana narrative in north India, Bastar Dussehra honors Goddess Danteshwari and is influenced by local goddess traditions, tribal involvement, and the historical heritage of the Bastar region.

Visitors must refrain from mistaking it for a version of Ravana Dahan.

Community labor and ritual participation

Community participation is essential in the success of large public festivals, with various forms of contribution such as processional work, ritual roles, preparation, music, craft, and collective coordination playing a crucial role. The celebration in Bastar highlights the significance of community involvement in transforming ordinary activities into sacred practices.

The event highlights the preservation of regional identity through recurring public ceremonies.

How to approach as a visitor

It is important for travelers to prioritize cultural respect, seek local guidance, and obtain consent before taking photographs. Ritual spaces should not be treated as staged performances, even if they are visited by outsiders.

A meaningful visit prioritizes listening and understanding over gathering souvenirs.

Common questions

Is Bastar Dussehra about Ravana Dahan?

The festival revolves around Goddess Danteshwari and the unique traditions of Bastar region.

Why is it described as 75 days?

It is a lengthy ritual cycle lasting about 75 days.

Where is it celebrated?

It is associated with the Bastar region of Chhattisgarh.
