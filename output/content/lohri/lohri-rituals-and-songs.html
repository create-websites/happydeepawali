Lohri Essentials
January 13

The Soul of Lohri

Lohri holds great symbolism, with traditions revolving around the sacred bonfire, heartfelt offerings, and traditional songs that convey appreciation, optimism, and unity among the community.

Gratitude & Renewal

Lohri, a festival observed in Punjab, Haryana, and other regions, marks the conclusion of winter and honors the Sun's movement towards the north. Families come together during this time to express gratitude for the harvest and foster relationships within their communities.

The Bonfire

At the core of the festival lies the embodiment of the Sun God and the revival of warmth.

Offerings

Gestures of thanksgiving using sesame, jaggery, and harvest produce.

Songs

Protecting the past and cultural heritage through oral traditions such as 'Sundar Mundariye Ho'.

The Central Ritual

The Sacred Fire

Symbol of the Sun

The fire is a symbol of the Sun God (Surya), revered for bringing warmth, vitality, and agricultural prosperity following the winter chill.

Renewal & Farewell

Kindling the bonfire marks the end of winter's grip and the start of lengthening days and the approaching harvest time.

Unity & Protection

The fire unites people, strengthening bonds and providing security. Families move around it in a clockwise direction, representing the eternal cycle of life.

Parikrama Ritual

Circling the bonfire, typically seven times, symbolizes reverence for nature and a desire for safeguarding, abundance, and well-being, elevating the bonfire to a sacred area of communal supplication.

Gratitude & Abundance

Offerings to the Fire

Necessary farming goods tossed into the flames as a token of gratitude.

Til (Sesame)

Symbolizes warmth, energy, and fertility.

Gur (Jaggery)

Represents sweetness, prosperity, and good fortune.

Harvest Produce

Sugarcane, popcorn, and peanuts symbolize abundance.

Preserving History Through Song

Folk songs serve as the oral tradition of Lohri, transmitting values across generations.

"Sundar Mundariye Ho"
Dulla Bhatti: Praising the folk hero of Punjab.
Blessings: For harvest, fertility, and prosperity.
Celebration: Of newborns and newlyweds.
Gratitude: Expressions of community joy.

Children's Rituals

Children go from house to house singing tunes and gathering treats, promoting the values of sharing and generosity. It is commonly believed that homes that give are blessed.

Newborns & Newlyweds

Additional ceremonies and melodies are conducted to invoke blessings upon fresh starts, representing the ongoing nature of familial bonds and shared happiness.
