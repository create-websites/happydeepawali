Govardhan Puja and Annakut

Govardhan Puja is strongly associated with Krishna traditions in parts of north and western India and often follows the main Diwali night. Many temples celebrate with Annakut, literally a "mountain of food," through elaborate offerings.

The Govardhan Story

A familiar Krishna tradition describes Krishna encouraging the people of Vraja to honor Govardhan and their local environment rather than performing a customary sacrifice to Indra. The story culminates in Krishna lifting Govardhan to protect the community from destructive rain.

Different texts and devotional traditions frame the episode in their own ways.

Home Celebration

For why sisters apply tilak on bhai dooj, a simple home observance may include:

  • a small Krishna image or Govardhan symbol;
  • flowers and diya;
  • food offerings;
  • devotional songs or reading;
  • family meal and prasad.

Some households create a symbolic Govardhan mound using cow dung, clay or food; local practice varies.

Annakut

Temple Annakut displays can contain many dishes arranged before the deity. At home, there is no need to imitate the scale of a large temple. A small number of thoughtfully prepared foods is sufficient.

Bali Pratipada and Padwa

The day after Diwali is also associated in some regions with Bali Pratipada or Padwa, linked to King Bali and regional marital or household traditions.

Gujarati New Year

In Gujarat, Bestu Varas marks the new year around this period. Families exchange greetings, visit temples and reconnect with relatives and business networks.

Bhai Dooj

The festival cycle concludes in many regions with Bhai Dooj/Bhau Beej, emphasizing sibling relationships through tilak, blessings, meals and gifts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Why Sisters Apply Tilak on Bhai Dooj the same everywhere?

No. Diwali and Deepavali traditions vary by region, religion, family and local calendar.

Does every family perform Lakshmi Puja?

No. Lakshmi Puja is very common, but other regions and religious communities may emphasize different observances.

Are fireworks required for Diwali?

No. Diwali can be celebrated through lamps, prayer, food, family gatherings, decor and community activities without fireworks.

How can I celebrate on a smaller budget?

Reuse decor, make a limited menu, exchange fewer gifts and focus on family traditions rather than shopping volume.

How do I get accurate puja timing?

Use a reliable panchang or temple source calculated for your location because timing can differ by city and time zone.
