Trick-or-Treating Through the Ages: Souling, Guising and Candy Runs

Trick-or-treating today may seem straightforward: put on a costume, go door-to-door, and gather treats. But its origins are far more intricate, blending traditional door-to-door practices with communal attempts to transform Halloween pranks into a organized family tradition.

Souling and seasonal visiting

During medieval and early modern Europe, poorer individuals and children would occasionally go door to door during All Souls' observances, requesting food in exchange for prayers or songs. These traditions differed depending on the region and should not be viewed as a direct precursor, but they indicate the long-standing practice of ritual visiting during the season.

Guising brings performance to the doorstep

In Scotland and Ireland, "guisers" donned disguises and entertained with songs, jokes, recitations, or tricks in exchange for food or coins, highlighting the importance of performance in the evolution of Halloween traditions.

The North American neighborhood ritual

As Halloween gained popularity in the United States and Canada, communities began hosting parties, parades, and children's activities. In the twentieth century, candy became more affordable and readily available, suburban neighborhoods grew, and trick-or-treating became the tradition we know today.

Making the tradition more inclusive

Numerous neighborhoods have adopted allergy-conscious treats, non-edible alternatives, sensory-friendly hours, and "trunk-or-treat" events to promote a safer and more inclusive Halloween experience for all families, while still embracing the tradition of neighbors welcoming children at their doors.

Frequently asked questions

When did “trick or treat” become a common phrase?

The term gained traction in North American newspapers and popular culture in the early 1900s, growing significantly in the postwar period.

Is trick-or-treating the same everywhere?

Timing, age expectations, candy customs, and community regulations differ greatly depending on the country, city, and neighborhood.
