Sustainable Spookiness: An Eco-Friendly Halloween Guide

Halloween is a great time to let your creativity shine, making it simple to cut down on waste. The most eco-friendly decorations and costumes are typically those made from items you already have, can borrow, mend, or reuse in the future.

Start with what already exists

Consider shopping your closet, swapping with friends, hitting up a thrift store, or getting creative with pieces from past years before splurging on a new costume. With some basic black clothing, an old sheet, cardboard, scarves, and accessories, you can easily transform into countless recognizable characters.

Decorate with reusable and compostable materials

Opt for fabric bunting, paper silhouettes, natural branches, pumpkins, gourds, and reusable lights instead of disposable plastic items. Organize durable decorations in labeled bins to create a yearly collection rather than a one-time purchase.

Make pumpkins part of the food cycle

If a carving pumpkin is free of contaminants, utilize its flesh or seeds as needed; if not, follow local guidelines for composting. Remember to remove any candles, paint, glitter, or artificial decorations before composting.

Rethink treats and energy use

Purchase candy in quantities that match the anticipated number of visitors to minimize excess, add some non-edible options, and utilize LED lights set on timers. Sustainability flourishes when it becomes a simple and repeatable practice annually.

Frequently asked questions

Are artificial decorations always worse than natural ones?

It's not always the case. Opting for a long-lasting decoration that can be reused for years may be better than constantly purchasing disposable options. The lifespan of the item is important.

Can painted pumpkins be composted?

It varies based on the type of paint and compost guidelines in your area, but plain, untreated pumpkin material is the best choice for composting.
