The day following Halloween frequently presents an odd pantry dilemma: an abundance of small candies with various flavors. Think of them as mix-ins for baking. Start by sorting them based on texture and taste, then mix together candies that are alike instead of tossing them all into one recipe.

Chop chocolate into baking mix-ins

Use mini chocolate bars instead of chocolate chips in cookies, blondies, or brownies. For easier cutting and less smearing, briefly freeze soft or caramel-filled pieces before chopping.

Build bark and snack mixes

To make a delicious treat, melt chocolate and spread it thinly, then sprinkle on chopped candy, pretzels, or nuts before it hardens. For a perfect balance of sweet and salty, try mixing popcorn with small candies.

Use candies as toppings, not just ingredients

Adding crushed candies on top of cupcakes, ice cream, milkshakes, or a basic sheet cake can provide a visually appealing finishing touch, while also helping to identify and separate potential allergens.

Store what you cannot use immediately

Numerous wrapped candies maintain their freshness when stored correctly, while certain baked goods can be frozen with good results. It is best to discard any unwrapped, damaged, or questionable treats instead of attempting to salvage them in a recipe.

Frequently asked questions

Can leftover candy be frozen?

Certain candies have better freezing capabilities than others. Always refer to the manufacturer's instructions and anticipate alterations in the texture of candies containing caramel, wafers, or fillings.

What is the easiest leftover candy recipe?

Chocolate bark and cookie mix-ins allow you to easily combine multiple candies without much prior preparation.
