Pairing candy and wine is most effective as a fun tasting activity rather than a strict set of guidelines. Key factors to consider are sweetness, acidity, chocolate strength, salt, and texture. Opt for small portions and select combinations that you find pleasurable rather than strictly adhering to rules.

Match sweetness before complexity

Dry wines may come across as abrasive when paired with sugary treats, whereas dessert wines, off-dry varieties, and fruit-forward options tend to complement sweet flavors better than tannic reds.

Chocolate likes different partners

Milk chocolate pairs well with softer, fruitier wines, while dark chocolate complements deeper flavors but may clash with high tannins. White chocolate, on the other hand, is rich and sweet, creating intriguing contrasts with aromatic or lightly sparkling styles.

Caramel, nuts and salt change the equation

Adding salt can help to reduce the bitterness, while the combination of caramel and peanut flavors can enhance nutty or oxidative nuances. Experiment by pairing a single candy with two small wine pours to observe how the flavors evolve.

Keep the tasting clearly adult

This activity is exclusively for adults who are of legal drinking age. Make sure to offer water, food, and nonalcoholic options, and avoid turning a fun pairing into a drinking obligation.

Dark chocolateRicher wine; watch high tannin

Frequently asked questions

Is there one best wine for Halloween candy?

Different candy recipes and personal preferences can lead to variations in taste. Consider using sweetness, acidity, and intensity as general guidelines instead of strict rules.

Can I run the same tasting without alcohol?

Sure, experiment with sparkling grape juice, tea, cold brew, spiced cider, or botanical non-alcoholic beverages to see how sweetness and acidity complement candy flavors.
