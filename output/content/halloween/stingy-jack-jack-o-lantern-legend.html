The association of the jack-o'-lantern with Halloween is undeniable, but its origins can be traced back to Irish folklore, where tales of the mischievous Stingy Jack sparked the tradition of carving lanterns that eventually made their way to America from across the sea.

Who was Stingy Jack?

There are various interpretations of the folklore surrounding Jack, but at its heart, the story depicts him as a cunning and self-serving trickster who outsmarts the Devil through bargains. Following his demise, Jack is unable to find peace and is doomed to wander aimlessly with only a glowing coal to light his way. Due to its oral nature, the tale has multiple versions and no definitive authority.

From wandering lights to carved faces

In Britain and Ireland, mysterious lights observed above marshes were occasionally attributed to tales of roaming spirits. Locals crafted lanterns out of turnips, mangel-wurzels, or other root vegetables, depicting eerie faces that added a playful, protective, or theatrical touch to autumn festivities.

Why pumpkins took over

Immigrants in North America were met with large, plentiful pumpkins that were easier to hollow out and carve. This led to the practical and visually striking pumpkin lantern. By the late 1800s and early 1900s, magazines, postcards, and community events helped establish the orange jack-o'-lantern as a Halloween symbol.

A tradition that keeps reinventing itself

Contemporary carving can range from basic geometric shapes to intricate portraits and sculptural creations, all rooted in the traditional elements of harvest, light, faces, darkness, and storytelling.

Frequently asked questions

Was Stingy Jack a historical person?

He should be regarded as a figure of folklore. Various renditions of the tale were passed down through word of mouth, evolving with each retelling.

Why are jack-o’-lanterns carved with scary faces?

Faces can draw from traditional masking and lantern practices, with contemporary variations that span from eerie to amusing and ornamental.
