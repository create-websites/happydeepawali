Global Easter Breads: Colomba, Babka and Tsoureki

Explore the traditions behind three festive breads - Italian colomba, Polish babka, and Greek tsoureki.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCulinary & Baking Trends

Colomba di Pasqua: the dove-shaped Italian bread

Colomba is a luxurious Italian Easter bread designed in the shape of a dove, often adorned with almonds and a crispy sugar coating. While inspired by the enriched dough of panettone, Colomba has evolved into its own distinctive Easter treat.

Polish babka: tall, rich and celebratory

Babka comes in various styles throughout Eastern Europe, with Polish Easter variations typically being tall, buttery cakes or yeast breads infused with citrus, vanilla, or raisins, and topped with glaze or sugar.

Greek tsoureki: aromatic braided bread

Tsoureki, a traditional Greek Easter bread, is a soft braided loaf typically flavored with mahlepi and sometimes mastic. It is common to see red-dyed eggs nestled within the braid as a symbolic Easter decoration.

Bake the tradition, not just the shape

Every type of bread is tied to a rich cultural and religious background. When trying a new bread, look for recipes from cooks who share the regional ingredients and family stories, rather than solely relying on its appearance.

Frequently asked questions

Are these breads interchangeable?

Yes, each has unique doughs, aromatics, shapes, and cultural histories, despite their common appearance at Easter.

Why are eggs sometimes baked into tsoureki?

Red eggs are frequently seen as a traditional Greek Easter symbol and can be used ornamentally in the woven bread.
