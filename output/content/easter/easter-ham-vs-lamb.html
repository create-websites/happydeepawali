Easter Ham vs. Lamb: Choosing the Holiday Centerpiece

Analyze two traditional Easter centerpieces in terms of taste, symbolism, preparation method, guest preferences, and leftover possibilities.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCulinary & Baking Trends

Why families choose ham

Ham is a common favorite for North American Easter feasts due to its ability to serve a large group, be made in advance, and complement a variety of sweet or tangy sauces. Plus, leftover bone-in ham can be repurposed for sandwiches, soups, and breakfast items.

Why families choose lamb

Lamb is deeply symbolic and widely associated with Passover and Easter in various cultures. Roasted leg or shoulder of lamb pairs perfectly with garlic, rosemary, lemon, and herbs, while those in smaller households may opt for chops.

Choose based on your table, not a rule

Consider guest preferences, oven capacity, budget constraints, and the selection of side dishes you plan to include. Instead of opting for a single large meat dish, a mixed table could feature a smaller roast alongside a vegetarian centerpiece.

Plan leftovers before you cook

Ham can transform into biscuits, strata, or bean soup; while lamb can be turned into sandwiches, grain bowls, or shepherd’s pie-style bakes. It is crucial to promptly cool and refrigerate leftovers rather than trying to stretch them for an extra meal.

Frequently asked questions

Is lamb more traditional than ham for Easter?

Lamb has historical religious connotations, whereas ham has become closely linked with Easter in numerous North American homes, both representing long-standing traditions.

Which is easier for a large group?

Ham is frequently easier to prepare since it requires less last-minute cooking when heated and glazed.
