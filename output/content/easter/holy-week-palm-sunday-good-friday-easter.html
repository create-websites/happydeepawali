Holy Week Explained: From Palm Sunday to Easter

An informative overview of Palm Sunday, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday, and Easter Sunday.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterHistory, Folklore & Spiritual Origins

Palm Sunday: entry into Jerusalem

Palm Sunday marks the beginning of Holy Week, commemorating Jesus' arrival in Jerusalem and the enthusiastic reception from the crowds. Some churches incorporate palm branches in their worship services, while others use local greenery as a substitute where palms are not readily available.

Maundy Thursday: meal, service and humility

Maundy Thursday commemorates the Last Supper and, in various customs, the act of Jesus washing the feet of his disciples. Observances may involve partaking in communion, foot washing, the symbolic stripping of the altar, or a solemn vigil that lasts through the night.

Good Friday and Holy Saturday

Good Friday centers on the crucifixion, with a solemn tone and traditions like Passion readings, veneration of the cross, and extended prayer. Holy Saturday is a day of anticipation that often culminates in the Easter Vigil after sunset in many churches.

Easter Sunday: resurrection and joy

Easter Sunday is a day of celebrating resurrection through music, flowers, light, and communal worship. From sunrise services to cathedral liturgies to small local gatherings, all reflect a transition from mourning to hope.

When attending a church for the first time, make sure to review service details and dress codes, as Holy Week customs can differ greatly between denominations and cultures.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Good Friday and Easter Sunday?

Good Friday commemorates the crucifixion; Easter Sunday celebrates the resurrection.

Why is Holy Week called holy?

It condenses the key moments of the Christian Passion story into a series of days dedicated to prayer and worship.
