Zero-Waste Easter Baskets: Practical Eco-Friendly Ideas

Create Easter baskets using reusable containers, practical gifts, and compostable filler instead of disposable plastic.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCrafts, Decor & DIY

Start with the container

Select a basket, storage bin, tote, garden pot, or fabric bag from your existing collection that can be repurposed after Easter. Reusing the container each year makes a significant difference, as it is typically the largest and most long-lasting part of the gift.

Replace plastic grass

Using shredded recycled paper, a scarf, cloth napkins, or crinkle-cut kraft paper can provide cushioning for gifts without the need for plastic strands. Fabric can also make it easier to unpack gifts around pets and toddlers.

Choose gifts that get used

Instead of novelty plastic, consider investing in books, art supplies, seed packets, sidewalk chalk, reusable water bottles, gardening tools, and experience coupons. Opt for buying favorite treats instead of a large variety that might not all be consumed.

Reuse the decor next year

Keep ribbons, wooden eggs, fabric bunting, and sturdy baskets stored together post-holiday. Using a labeled Easter box will help curb the urge to purchase new decorations each spring.

Frequently asked questions

What can replace plastic Easter grass?

Shredded paper, fabric, cloth napkins, and reusable scarves are all effective options.

Can a zero-waste basket still include candy?

Yes, the objective is to reduce waste, prioritize treats that are enjoyable, and minimize unnecessary packaging as much as possible.
