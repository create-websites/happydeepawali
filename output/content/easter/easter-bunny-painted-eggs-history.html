The Easter Bunny and Painted Eggs: Folklore Explained

Follow the history of the rabbit, egg decorations, and gift-giving customs that influenced the development of the modern Easter basket.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterHistory, Folklore & Spiritual Origins

Why eggs became an Easter symbol

Eggs have long been connected to life and rebirth, serving as a seasonal symbol. In Christian Europe, eggs were not only symbolic but also practical, as fasting traditions during Lent often limited certain foods, making decorated or gifted eggs particularly significant at the end of the fast.

Various regions have unique methods for dyeing, scratching, waxing, and painting Easter eggs, resulting in a lack of a singular origin for the tradition.

From hares to a gift-bringing bunny

German-speaking traditions played a significant role in popularizing tales of an Easter hare known as the Osterhase, who would assess children and distribute colorful eggs. These customs were brought to North America by immigrants, leading to the transformation of the hare into the more gentle and child-appropriate Easter Bunny.

Baskets developed from nests, with eggs accompanied by candy, toys, and little spring surprises.

Why the custom endured

The tradition of celebrating Easter is flexible. Families have the option to turn the bunny into a fun figure, a guide for scavenger hunts, a decoration, or even disregard it altogether. Similarly, the egg can hold religious significance, be used for artistic purposes, be cooked into a culinary delight, or simply serve as a seasonal symbol, all depending on the preferences of each

Build a tradition with context

When celebrating with kids, make sure to include a brief explanation: Easter traditions stem from church practices, folklore, and modern commercial culture, showing how customs have changed over time.

Frequently asked questions

Did the Easter Bunny begin in Germany?

The custom of the gift-bringing hare is closely linked to German-speaking regions of Europe and has since spread through migration and popular culture.

Why are eggs decorated at Easter?

Egg decoration combines seasonal symbolism, Christian fasting practices, and local folk art customs.
