Stress-Free Easter Hosting: A Simple Timeline

Create a plan for an Easter dinner with a practical schedule for preparation, dishes that can be made in advance, a versatile table setting, and a more relaxed approach to hosting.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

One week before: decide the shape of the day

Select between brunch, lunch, or dinner, confirm the number of guests attending, inquire about dietary restrictions, and determine if the meal will be plated, buffet-style, or family-style. This choice will greatly impact all future decisions.

Two to three days before: do the invisible work

Shop for ingredients, wash serving pieces, prepare dressings and sauces, bake desserts, and set out beverages. Also, arrange table decor excluding fresh flowers or food.

The day before: reduce oven traffic

Prepare casseroles, dice vegetables, arrange table settings, and clear space in the fridge for the holiday feast. Create an oven timetable to ensure each dish is cooked perfectly without overcrowding the temperature.

Easter day: protect a margin

Strive to get the kitchen in order before guests show up. Set out a simple snack, allow guests to pitch in with drinks, and be okay with one dish looking less than perfect. A laid-back host often enhances the gathering more than a flawless centerpiece.

Frequently asked questions

What should I make ahead for Easter?

Desserts, dressings, cold salads, casseroles, and various side-dish components can frequently be made a day or two in advance.

Is brunch easier than dinner?

The best format for your gathering will depend on your schedule and guests, but using make-ahead bakes, fruit, and simple breads can be especially helpful.
