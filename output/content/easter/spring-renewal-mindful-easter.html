Spring Renewal: A Mindful Easter Reset

Let the change in seasons gently encourage you to declutter, spend time outdoors, reflect, and cultivate healthy habits.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

Choose a small physical reset

To tackle spring-cleaning, focus on clearing one visible area like a front entry, kitchen counter, or bedroom chair instead of emptying every closet. Completing a small area fully will give you more motivation than leaving several rooms partially done.

Reconnect with daylight and movement

The longer daylight hours provide more opportunities to enjoy activities like taking an evening stroll, gardening, meeting up with friends outdoors, or exercising outside. Embrace the season as a time for leisure rather than setting strict performance goals.

Make room for reflection

Religious families find themes of hope, renewal, and new beginnings in Easter, while secular families can still take advantage of the seasonal change to reflect on routines, relationships, and priorities without needing to use theological language that may not resonate with them.

Design one habit that survives April

Select something simple yet sustainable to incorporate into your routine: placing fresh flowers on the table, taking a screen-free walk each week, setting up a donation box near the door, or planning meals on Sundays. Consistency is key in making renewal a lasting habit.

Frequently asked questions

Is a spring reset required to undertake a significant decluttering effort?

Completing one area or adopting a new routine can be more beneficial than attempting a complete overhaul of your entire house.

Can this idea work for nonreligious households?

Certainly. Transitioning into a new season can be done practically without incorporating religious terminology.
