White House Easter Egg Roll: History and Traditions

Follow the evolution of the South Lawn tradition, dating back to 1878, through music, egg races, souvenir eggs, and contemporary family activities.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

Why the White House lawn became the setting

Egg rolling on Easter Monday was a well-loved tradition in Washington, but when Congress limited access to the U.S. Capitol grounds, President Rutherford B. Hayes stepped in and allowed children to roll eggs on the White House lawn in 1878, creating the annual White House Easter Egg Roll.

A tradition that kept evolving

Over time, the U.S. Marine Band joined the festivities in the nineteenth century, adding music, games, and public appearances. Egg-roll races eventually became a popular signature activity.

Souvenir eggs and family programming

The Reagan White House launched a search for wooden eggs autographed by famous individuals in 1981. These painted eggs became cherished mementos, and over time, the event evolved to feature storytelling, live performances, and interactive stations.

Why the event matters beyond politics

The Egg Roll, a long-standing family tradition held at a political landmark, continues to draw children to the South Lawn for spring games despite challenges such as war, renovation, weather, and public-health emergencies.

When was the first annual White House Easter Egg Roll?

The White House Historical Association established the tradition of the first annual White House Easter Egg Roll on April 22, 1878.

Who opened the White House grounds to egg rollers?

In 1878, President Rutherford B. Hayes allowed children to access the grounds.
