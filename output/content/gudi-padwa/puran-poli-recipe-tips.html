Mastering Puran Poli for Gudi Padwa

Learn the necessary skills for making soft and thin puran poli, including cooking the chana dal filling and rolling it without tearing.

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

The cuisine of Maharashtrian New Year blends tradition, warmth, and local flavors. From the beloved puran poli to lesser-known dishes and portable gifts, these culinary traditions offer a complete experience.

The two-part challenge

To make Puran poli successfully, you must achieve the perfect balance of textures: a smooth, cohesive filling and a soft, elastic outer dough that can stretch around it without becoming watery.

If one component is too dry, the dough will crack. If one is too moist, rolling becomes difficult.

Cook the dal until tender, not mushy

Chana dal should be cooked until fully tender, yet still drainable. It is important to remove excess water to ensure a smooth filling; therefore, make sure to drain thoroughly before incorporating jaggery as per your recipe instructions.

After the jaggery has melted, the mixture will become loose again. Keep cooking and stirring patiently until it thickens to a point where it can hold its shape. Be sure to stir often to avoid burning.

Make the puran silky

In traditional cooking, the cooked filling is typically strained through a puran yantra or another tool to ensure a uniform texture. Alternatively, any kitchen method that results in a smooth, clump-free filling can be used.

Add a touch of cardamom and, if desired, a sprinkle of nutmeg to enhance the flavor. Allow the filling to cool before filling the dough.

Use a soft, rested dough

The dough should have a softer consistency compared to a traditional roti dough. Different families use different types of flour, some even combine flours, and methods vary by region. Allowing the dough to rest helps evenly distribute moisture and facilitates easier stretching.

Oil or ghee can be utilized as per the recipe to achieve elasticity without any stickiness.

Stuff generously, roll gently

A well-made puran poli is typically filled generously with a smooth ball of puran inside flattened dough, sealed carefully, rolled with light pressure, and rotated frequently instead of forcing the pin in one direction.

Use the appropriate amount of dusting flour to avoid drying out the surface with excess flour.

Cook until spotted and fragrant

Cook the poli on a properly heated tawa until bubbles and light brown spots appear on the surface. Be careful not to overcook, as it can make the poli leathery. Serve while warm.

The initial one or two attempts may not be flawless. Use them as a guide to adjust heat, filling consistency, or dough softness before continuing with the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Why does puran poli tear while rolling?

Dry dough, lumpy filling, excessively wet filling, or excessive rolling pressure are frequently to blame.

Can puran be made ahead?

Indeed, the filling is frequently made in advance, cooled, stored in the refrigerator, and then brought to a suitable temperature for use.

Why is my puran poli hard?

If the dough is overcooked, too stiff, or has an excess of dusting flour, it may become tough instead of soft.
